\begin{abstract}
Long-horizon LLM agents need memory that persists across sessions, organizes
itself under use, forgets gracefully, and serves retrieval at low cost.
The last year of research has converged on multi-relational memory graphs,
retrieval-driven consolidation, and the separation of memory control from
generation. Yet the surveyed systems predominantly store memories as
Euclidean vectors, compare them with cosine similarity, and index them
with Euclidean approximate nearest-neighbor structures. Curved geometry has touched retrieval-augmented generation \cite{hyperbolicrag} and unreviewed engineering projects, but not---to our knowledge---the full lifecycle of agent memory with formal mechanisms. This paper proposes
\textbf{GyroMem}, a theory of agentic memory in which memories live on a
\emph{curved} manifold: a product of hyperbolic, spherical, and Euclidean
components with curvatures to be fixed per memory region in the specification.
Retrieval becomes geodesic distance; consolidation becomes geodesic
transport of memory points toward useful queries, with confidence-bounded
step sizes and a stability sketch for the hyperbolic component only;
forgetting becomes geometric drift of availability rather than deletion;
and the transient (KV-cache) and persistent (external store) tiers are
managed under a single geometric budget. We formalize the theory, state the
exact gyrovector operations it requires, state a stability sketch for the
reconsolidation update, and pre-register a falsification-oriented
experimental program---including explicit kill criteria---under which the
theory should be abandoned or revised. This is a theory paper: we claim no
empirical results for GyroMem itself. Our contribution is a geometric
reframing of agentic memory, stated precisely enough to falsify---implementation
parameters are named as specification parameters to be fixed in the
companion specification (see the specification note in
\S\ref{sec:manifold})---plus an honest accounting of where it is most
likely to fail.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} agentic memory, hyperbolic geometry, product
manifolds, retrieval-augmented generation, memory consolidation, geodesic
reconsolidation

\section{Introduction}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

An LLM agent that cannot remember is an amnesiac savant: brilliant within a
context window, a stranger to everything before it. As agents take on
long-horizon work---software projects spanning weeks, personal assistants
spanning years, scientific collaborators spanning careers---memory stops
being a feature and becomes the substrate. The research community has
responded with remarkable energy: in a single recent month (September--October
2026), at least twelve papers advanced agentic memory, from control-plane
architectures to KV-cache eviction to consolidation governance
\cite{jevmem,jam,realm,actkv,epistemics,mhr,engramrag,biomem,mrvq,agentkv,collabmem,simplearm}.

Stepping back from this burst of activity, a pattern---and a conspicuous
absence---emerges. The pattern: memory is becoming a \emph{closed-loop
lifecycle} rather than a store. Retrieval rewires the graph
\cite{realm,engramrag}; context is constructed just-in-time at query time
rather than pre-compressed \cite{jam}; memory control is being split off
from generation into cheaper dedicated machinery \cite{jevmem}; forgetting
is becoming a first-class, principled design variable \cite{engramrag,
epistemics}. The absence: \emph{the geometry rarely changed.} Predominantly,
the systems surveyed store memories as Euclidean vectors, score them
with cosine similarity (or fusions thereof), and index them with
Euclidean ANN structures (HNSW, PQ). The exceptions---graph systems, a
page-store, and hash-code indexes---are named where the survey qualifies
this claim below. The vector mathematics of memory---the space itself
in which memories live, move, and decay---has been treated as fixed
plumbing while all the innovation happened one level up.

This paper asks whether the plumbing is the problem. Agent memory is
simultaneously \emph{hierarchical} (identity $\to$ preferences $\to$
episodes $\to$ raw facts), \emph{relational} (entities linked in cycles and
mutual reference), and \emph{flat} (surface feature similarity). Euclidean
space is a poor host for the first of these: trees embed into Euclidean
space only with severe distortion, while hyperbolic space accommodates them
with arbitrarily low distortion \cite{nickel2017}. The mismatch is not
aesthetic. If memories live in the wrong geometry, then every downstream
operation---retrieval ranking, consolidation updates, forgetting
schedules---must compensate with heuristics, thresholds, and expensive
LLM-driven repairs for what the geometry gets wrong for free.

\textbf{Thesis.} \emph{Agentic memory should live on a curved manifold: a
product of hyperbolic, spherical, and Euclidean components with
per-region curvature to be fixed in the specification. Retrieval is geodesic
distance. Consolidation is geodesic transport. Forgetting is geometric
drift of availability. The resulting system may be cheaper than LLM-driven
memory surgery and more stable than heuristic decay; both claims are
hypotheses tested by the pre-registered program in
\S\ref{sec:experiments}.}

We name this theory \textbf{GyroMem}, after the gyrovector spaces that
furnish its algebra. Our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item A formal specification of a product-manifold memory space for LLM
agents, with explicit formation (exponential map), retrieval (geodesic
distance), consolidation (confidence-bounded geodesic transport),
promotion (radial motion), and forgetting (availability drift) operators.
\item A stability sketch for the reconsolidation update on the hyperbolic
component, giving the kind of convergence argument that usage-driven
plasticity in current systems lacks.
\item A unification of the transient (KV-cache) and persistent (external)
memory tiers under one geometric budget, addressing a split no current
system bridges.
\item A pre-registered, falsification-oriented experimental program with
named hypotheses, ablations that isolate each geometric claim, and
explicit kill criteria---so the theory can fail fast and honestly.
\item A candid failure analysis: where the theory is most likely wrong,
what prior art constrains it, and what a negative result would still teach.
\end{enumerate}

We emphasize what this paper is \emph{not}: it reports no experiments on
GyroMem, claims no benchmark victories, and invents no data. It is a theory
paper written \emph{before} the experiments, in the open, so that the
subsequent empirical program---pass or fail---can be judged against
pre-committed predictions rather than retrofitted narratives.

\section{Background and Related Work}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Agentic memory systems: the state of play}

The recent literature clusters into several lines. \textbf{Control-plane
architectures} separate memory decisions from generation: Jev-Mem routes
all high-frequency memory decisions (typing, routing, budget allocation,
traversal, stopping) through a lightweight typed-probabilistic controller,
reserving the LLM for synthesis, reaching 0.777 on LoCoMo as reported in
\cite{jevmem} (executor, judge, and confidence interval not restated
here; the figure is an LLM-judge score, GPT-4o-mini, as reported via
secondary summaries).
\textbf{Just-in-time memory} rejects ahead-of-time compression: JAM preserves raw histories in a hierarchical
page-store and performs runtime agentic research per query, with a 4B model
outperforming 7--14B trained memory agents across most settings as reported
in \cite{jam}---note the qualifier: the construction is query-conditioned,
not verbatim preservation of every history.
\textbf{Retrieval-driven reconsolidation} treats recall as a write event:
REALM applies LLM-driven edge surgery (add/strengthen/weaken with
confidence-bounded updates) on a heterogeneous cognitive graph after
retrieval \cite{realm}; EngramRAG implements a waking/dreaming cycle with
Hebbian edge plasticity, usage-modulated PageRank, and topology-aware decay
\cite{engramrag}. \textbf{KV-cache management} optimizes the transient
tier: ActKV exploits stable action-critical attention patterns
\cite{actkv}, while AgentKV models the phase-mixture of query subspaces
\cite{agentkv}. \textbf{Index elasticity} arrives via Matryoshka-nested
codes serving multiple operating points from one artifact
\cite{mhr,mrvq,kusupati2022}.
\textbf{Governance and evaluation} mature: the Epistemics program exposes
degenerate forgetting and argues that consolidation-quality metrics do not
predict real transfer, proposing governed consolidation with
poison-resistance, reversibility, and auditability \cite{epistemics};
no correlation figure is quoted here, the reported figure having not been
verified for this paper, and the Epistemics experiments were designed,
executed, and adversarially verified by the PRIMA multi-agent system with
certain mechanism internals held under controlled release---a
reproducibility limitation on every Epistemics-derived claim in this
paper;
Bio-Memory conditions retrieval on biometric identity \cite{biomem}.

Predominant in the surveyed wave, though not universal---REALM and
EngramRAG are graph systems, JAM is a page-store, MHR/MRVQ use hash
codes---are Euclidean embeddings scored by cosine similarity and indexed
by Euclidean ANN. Within this wave, the geometry is rarely varied, rarely
ablated, and rarely questioned as a design variable.

\subsection{Hyperbolic representation learning}

The case for non-Euclidean representation is well established outside
agentic memory. Nickel and Kiela showed that hierarchical data---taxonomies,
trees, entailment cones---embeds into the Poincar\'e ball with lower
distortion than into Euclidean space of the same dimension in their
experiments, because volume in hyperbolic space grows exponentially with
radius, matching the exponential growth of tree nodes with depth
\cite{nickel2017}. Ganea,
B\'ecigneul, and Hofmann extended the toolkit to hyperbolic neural
networks, defining M\"obius versions of addition, matrix multiplication,
and recurrent updates, with closed-form exponential and logarithmic maps
\cite{ganea2018}. Gu et al.\ generalized further to \emph{product
manifolds}---Cartesian products of hyperbolic, spherical, and Euclidean
components with learned curvatures---showing that heterogeneous data is
best served by heterogeneous geometry on static embedding tasks
\cite{gu2019}; the extrapolation from static embedding tasks to online
agent memory is this paper's hypothesis, not Gu et al.'s finding. These results are our
direct mathematical foundation; we apply them to the full lifecycle of
\emph{agent} memory rather than to static embedding tasks. The nearest
existing efforts are discussed in \S\ref{sec:nearest}.

\subsection{Nearest-neighbor search beyond Euclidean space}
\label{sec:related-ann}

If memories live on a manifold, retrieval needs manifold-aware search.
Hyperbolic nearest-neighbor methods exist but are immature relative to
FAISS/HNSW. Graph-based search has been reported to enjoy \emph{lower} time
complexity in hyperbolic than in Euclidean space in the dense regime
\cite{prokhorenkova2022}---a result reported in that paper, not verified
for this paper---and a hyperbolic-native LSH construction has been
reported recently \cite{deng2026}---likewise not verified here. The working engineering
pattern in the surveyed literature is two-stage Euclidean pre-filtering with hyperbolic reranking. Tangent-space approximations that map neighborhoods into the
tangent plane at the query point complete the toolkit. We treat ANN on the
product manifold as an engineering problem with known partial solutions,
not as a solved one---a risk we carry explicitly into \S\ref{sec:failure}.

\subsection{Curved geometry for retrieval and memory: the nearest neighbors}
\label{sec:nearest}

Cao et al.\ introduce HyperbolicRAG, embedding graph-RAG nodes in a shared
Poincar\'e manifold with mutual-ranking fusion of Euclidean and hyperbolic
signals, reported to improve multi-hop QA over Euclidean baselines
\cite{hyperbolicrag}---no dataset, model, margin, or baseline is restated
here, none having been verified for this paper---but it is retrieval-only: no memory lifecycle, no
reconsolidation, no forgetting, single curvature. Chakrabarti projects
pretrained embeddings into the Lorentz model for edge-device RAG reranking
\cite{chakrabarti2026}; no result is quoted here. No verified
product-manifold agent-memory system was found in the surveyed
literature: the one candidate known to us, a GitHub engineering project
implementing long-term agent memory on a product manifold, could not be
verified---its repository URL returned an error when checked in November
2026---and none of its reported claims is relied on here. To our knowledge, no peer-reviewed
work combines product-manifold geometry with retrieval-driven
reconsolidation, availability drift, and unified tier budgeting for
agent memory. GyroMem's contribution is therefore (i) the first formal
treatment of curved-geometry agent memory, with (ii) three mechanisms
absent from all prior art we found: geodesic reconsolidation with a
contraction-stability argument for the hyperbolic component,
curvature-parameterized availability drift, and tier-unified budgeting---plus (iii) a pre-registered
falsification program. The novelty claim is hedged: it is a claim about
the surveyed literature, and a single counterexample would narrow it.

Two newer results condition the program. A controlled diagnostic-task
test of geometric memory features found no added predictive value over
question-difficulty and system-identity baselines ($\Delta$AUROC $+0.002$,
95\% CI $[-0.010, 0.014]$, pooled AUROC 0.640 vs.\ 0.655 difficulty
baseline, $n = 2{,}367$; on single-session-assistant questions the
geometric classifier reached 0.841 vs.\ 0.826 for system identity alone)
\cite{geometry}---but it tested embedding proximity on a diagnostic task,
not curved or product-manifold geometry as a retrieval policy, so it
constrains the diagnostic claim, not the retrieval-policy claim tested in
H1. And explicit structure keeps winning where geometry is untested:
TIDER's temporal constraint channels beat semantic retrieval on LifeBench
and LoCoMo \cite{tider}, and an anti-persistence update rule that deletes
error-reproducing memories rather than reinforcing them is the strongest
rival updater to the transport rule of \S\ref{sec:reconsolidation}
\cite{antipersistence}. Both are named baselines in the experimental
program.

\subsection{Neuroscience: complementary systems and reconsolidation}

Two ideas from neuroscience recur, usually as loose metaphor, across the
agent-memory literature; we attempt to make them literal. Complementary
Learning Systems theory holds that the hippocampus (fast, episodic) and
neocortex (slow, semantic) consolidate via replay \cite{mcclelland1995}---the
template for waking/dreaming splits \cite{engramrag}. Memory
reconsolidation holds that recalled memories enter a labile state in which
they can be strengthened, weakened, or rewritten \cite{nader2000}---the
template for REALM's post-retrieval updates \cite{realm}. GyroMem's
geodesic transport is reconsolidation made geometric: the labile state is
motion along the manifold, and its bounds are step sizes, not prompts.

\subsection{The evaluation problem}

A theory of memory is only as good as its measures, and the measures are
troubled. LoCoMo with LLM-as-a-judge dominates evaluation, yet the
Epistemics program reports that well-constructed consolidation-quality
scores do not predict real transfer accuracy \cite{epistemics}, and
retrieval-metric wins need not transfer to downstream generation
\cite{engramrag}. Any honest experimental program must therefore measure
\emph{transfer}---whether consolidated memory changes what the agent can
do---not just recall. We build this lesson into our pre-registration
(\S\ref{sec:experiments}).

\section{Motivation: four gaps}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

From the survey above we distill four gaps that GyroMem is designed to
close. Each is a falsifiable claim about the literature, and each maps to
one component of the theory.

\textbf{Gap 1: the geometry is fixed and unexamined.} No system in the
surveyed wave varies the embedding geometry as a design variable; cosine similarity on Euclidean
vectors is predominant there. Curved geometry exists at the edges---retrieval-only
systems \cite{hyperbolicrag,chakrabarti2026}---but not as a formal, evaluated theory of the memory lifecycle. Yet agent memory is conspicuously hierarchical, the exact regime
where hyperbolic geometry dominates in static embedding experiments \cite{nickel2017}. A controlled diagnostic-task test found geometric features add nothing over difficulty and system-identity baselines \cite{geometry}; H1 answers it by testing geometry as a retrieval policy, not a diagnostic. \emph{Component:}
product-manifold memory space (\S\ref{sec:manifold}).

\textbf{Gap 2: plasticity without theory.} Usage-driven updates---Hebbian
rules, topology-aware decay, LLM graph surgery---are hand-tuned or
prompt-driven, with no convergence or stability analysis. Contrast the
rigorous capacity theory of classical associative memory. \emph{Component:}
geodesic reconsolidation with a contraction argument (\S\ref{sec:reconsolidation}).

\textbf{Gap 3: two tiers, no joint management.} KV-cache systems
\cite{actkv,agentkv} and external-memory systems \cite{jevmem,realm,jam}
optimize disjoint budgets though they compete for the same serving
resources. \emph{Component:} unified geometric budget (\S\ref{sec:tiers}).

\textbf{Gap 4: forgetting as an afterthought.} Decay is wall-clock
exponential (EngramRAG's own time term notwithstanding, pure wall-clock
decay is the wrong retention rule \cite{engramrag}), thresholded
hand-tuning, or---worst---a budget release valve that destroys load-bearing
memories \cite{epistemics}. \emph{Component:} forgetting as availability
drift (\S\ref{sec:forgetting}).

\section{The GyroMem Theory}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{The product-manifold memory space}
\label{sec:manifold}

\begin{definition}[Memory manifold]
Let
\begin{equation}
\M = \Hyp^{a}_{-c_h} \times \Sph^{b}_{c_s} \times \R^{c},
\end{equation}
the Cartesian product of a hyperbolic component of dimension $a$ and
curvature $-c_h < 0$, a spherical component of dimension $b$ and curvature
$c_s > 0$, and a Euclidean component of dimension $c$. Each memory $m$ is a
point $m = (m_h, m_s, m_e) \in \M$. Curvatures $c_h, c_s$ and the weights
$\alpha_h, \alpha_s, \alpha_e$ in the distance below are \emph{to be fixed} in
the specification---chosen per region at design time, not learned during
operation---following the product-manifold form of \cite{gu2019}, whose
learned-curvature result is on static embedding tasks. This paper states
one manifold, not a family that changes under use.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Specification status]
References to ``the specification'' in this paper mean the companion
GyroMem specification document, to be released with the prototype (see
the engineering roadmap). The specification does not yet exist. It will
fix the implementation parameters this paper names but does not value:
the component dimensions $a, b, c$; the curvature values per region; the
antipode tie-break rule; the formation-clustering algorithm and
parameters; the radial-regularizer radius targets and the abstraction
labeling procedure; the drift function $\lambda(u)$ and the drift rates;
the horizon radius $r_H$; the skill threshold
$r_{\mathrm{skill}}$; the tier-utility estimator; the just-in-time
initial radius $r_0$, threshold $\tau$, and candidate-set cap; and the
transient-tier timescale. Until the specification exists, two
implementers following this paper alone would build different systems.
The implementability claim of this paper is therefore limited to
falsifiability, as stated in the Abstract: every ``to be fixed in the
specification'' in this paper names a parameter to be fixed there before
any implementation, and no implementation claim in this paper rests on a
value the paper does not state.
\end{remark}

The three components have distinct duties. The \textbf{hyperbolic
component} hosts hierarchical content: identity facts near the origin,
preferences further out, episodes near the boundary---so that abstraction
level is literally radial position. The \textbf{spherical component}
hosts relational content: entity--entity associations and temporal cycles,
where closure (returning to the start) is natural. The \textbf{Euclidean
component} hosts flat featural content where cosine-style similarity is
already adequate. Distance on $\M$ is the weighted product distance
\begin{equation}
d_{\M}(p, q)^2 = \alpha_h\, d_{-c_h}(p_h, q_h)^2 + \alpha_s\, d_{c_s}(p_s, q_s)^2 + \alpha_e\, \|p_e - q_e\|^2,
\end{equation}
with fixed non-negative weights $\alpha_h, \alpha_s, \alpha_e$ (absorbed
into the component scales; we set $\alpha_h = \alpha_s = \alpha_e = 1$ in
the specification and vary only the curvatures across regions).
A \emph{region} is a subset of memories sharing curvature parameters and
weights (e.g., ``user preferences,'' ``project episodes''); regions are
formed by clustering encoder vectors \emph{before} formation, in Euclidean
space, by a clustering procedure whose algorithm and parameters are to be fixed
in the specification. The product distance above is the single distance
on $\M$: there is no separate cross-region rule, and no region-local
metric. Regions may split or merge only by re-clustering under the
dreaming consolidation described in \S\ref{sec:forgetting}, under criteria
to be fixed in the specification.

\begin{remark}[Why products, not pure hyperbolic]
Pure hyperbolic memory would repeat the monoculture it criticizes.
Relational cycles embed poorly in hyperbolic space and naturally in
spherical space; flat features need no curvature at all. The product
follows \cite{gu2019}: let the data's heterogeneity choose the geometry.
If ablations show one component dominates, the theory simplifies---that
is a feature, not a retreat.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Memory formation: the exponential map}
\label{sec:formation}

New memories arrive as Euclidean vectors (encoder outputs). Formation maps
them onto $\M$ via the exponential map at the origin of each component.
The encoder vector $v$ is split into components $v = (v_h, v_s, v_e)$ by a
fixed linear split whose dimensions $a, b, c$ are to be fixed in the
specification. For the Poincar\'e ball $\B^n_{c}$ of curvature $-c$, with M\"obius
addition $\oplus_c$ (formulary in Appendix~\ref{app:formulary}),
\begin{equation}
\exp^c_0(v) = \tanh(\sqrt{c}\,\|v\|)\,\frac{v}{\sqrt{c}\,\|v\|},
\qquad
\log^c_0(y) = \operatorname{artanh}(\sqrt{c}\,\|y\|)\,\frac{y}{\sqrt{c}\,\|y\|}.
\end{equation}
The spherical component is formed by normalizing the encoder slice to the
unit sphere, $m_s = v_s / \|v_s\|$, with a fixed north-pole base point;
spherical exponential and logarithmic maps are the standard great-circle
maps at the base point, and antipodal points (distance $\pi$) are resolved
by a fixed tie-break rule to be fixed in the specification. The product
exponential and logarithmic maps are component-wise: $\exp_m$ applies the
component maps at $m_h, m_s, m_e$ respectively, and likewise for $\log_m$.
The norm of the pre-image $v$ controls radial placement: small $\|v\|$
lands near the origin (abstract), large $\|v\|$ near the boundary
(specific). Formation therefore requires a \emph{calibrated} encoder norm,
which we obtain by training the encoder with a radial regularizer that
aligns $\|v\|$ with an abstraction label (identity $<$ preference $<$
episodic $<$ verbatim). The regularizer loss is stated explicitly:
$L_{\mathrm{rad}} = (\|v_h\| - r(\mathrm{label}))^2$ with increasing radius
targets $r(\cdot)$ to be fixed in the specification. The abstraction labels come
from a human or LLM-judge labeling procedure to be stated in the specification;
H2 therefore tests the judge and the geometry jointly, and that confound
is stated, not hidden. This is the one trained component of the otherwise
training-free theory, and its necessity is itself a falsifiable claim
(H2 in \S\ref{sec:experiments}).

\subsection{Retrieval as geodesic distance}
\label{sec:retrieval}

\begin{definition}[GyroMem retrieval]
Given query $q \in \M$ (formed as in \S\ref{sec:formation}), return the
$k$ memories minimizing $d_{\M}(q, m)$ under the single product distance.
Per-region top-$k$ union with reciprocal-rank fusion is an optional
variant, with the fusion rule $s(m) = \sum_r 1/(k_0 + \mathrm{rank}_r(m))$,
$k_0 = 60$, stated here; the default is the single ranking.
\end{definition}

Hyperbolic distance expands near the boundary of the ball: with
$\rho = \sqrt{c}\,\|v\| \in [0,1)$ the normalized radius of $v$,
$d_c(0,v) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\ln\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}$, so
$d_c(0,v) \approx \frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\ln\frac{2}{1-\rho}$ as
$\rho \to 1$ (natural logarithm; the ball radius in unnormalized
coordinates is $1/\sqrt{c}$). A query formed at the abstraction
level of ``preferences'' is therefore geodesically near the preference
region and far from individual episodes \emph{if} the encoder norm is
calibrated (H2) and angular separation holds---hierarchical pre-filtering
is a hypothesis conditional on H2, not a consequence of the metric alone.
Spherical distance is great-circle distance on the unit sphere. No claim
about hub distortion is made: none was measured or derived here.

\subsection{Reconsolidation as geodesic transport}
\label{sec:reconsolidation}

This is the load-bearing mechanism. Where REALM performs LLM-driven graph
surgery after retrieval \cite{realm}, GyroMem moves points.

\begin{definition}[Geodesic reconsolidation]
Let $m \in \M$ be a retrieved memory, $q \in \M$ the query (or the
retrieved context centroid), and $\kappa \in [0,1]$ a confidence score for the
retrieval's usefulness (downstream answer entailment, or a learned critic
whose computation cost is counted in H3). The update is
\begin{equation}
m \leftarrow \exp_m\!\big(\eta\,\kappa \cdot \log_m(q)\big)
\qquad\text{(strengthen)},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
m \leftarrow \exp_m\!\big(-\eta\,\kappa \cdot \log_m(q')\big)
\qquad\text{(weaken, against contradicted content $q'$)},
\end{equation}
with step size $\eta \in (0,1)$, where $q'$ is the point formed from the
contradicting evidence by the formation rule of
\S\ref{sec:formation}, and antipodal spherical targets are resolved by
the tie-break rule of \S\ref{sec:formation}. In the Poincar\'e component
the strengthen update is M\"obius interpolation toward $q$,
$m \leftarrow m \oplus_c \big(t \otimes_c ((-m) \oplus_c q)\big)$ with
$t = \eta\kappa$; scalar multiplication alone scales from the origin and
does not interpolate toward $q$. In the Euclidean
component it reduces to the familiar $m \leftarrow (1-\eta \kappa)m + \eta \kappa\,q$.
\end{definition}

The update is $O(d)$ per memory---one logarithmic and one exponential
map---versus an LLM call per edge in surgery-based approaches. It is a
bounded, confidence-gated update of the same family as REALM's $w \leftarrow
\mathrm{Clip}(w + \eta \kappa)$; no claim that it is a precise analogue of
that rule, or that it preserves topology, is made here.

\begin{proposition}[Stability sketch: hyperbolic component only]
Fix a target $q$ and consider the strengthen map
$T(m) = \exp_m(t \cdot \log_m(q))$ with $t = \eta \kappa \in [0,1)$ on a
Hadamard manifold (simply connected, non-positive curvature; the
hyperbolic component qualifies, the spherical component does not, and the
product manifold as a whole is not Hadamard). Then $T$ is a contraction toward
$q$: $d(T(m), q) = (1-t)\,d(m,q)$, strict for $t > 0$ (at $t = 0$, $T$ is
the identity). Iterating $T$ with the target fixed
converges to $q$ geometrically. No claim is made about query-varying
targets, stochastic convergence, or the spherical component: the
confidence sequence $\kappa_t$ is data-dependent, not a Robbins--Monro
schedule, and convergence to any Fr\'echet mean is a conjecture, stated
as such, whose proof would require a step-size schedule, compact-support
and bounded-curvature hypotheses, and a treatment of the spherical
component---none of which is supplied here.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The contraction identity is the defining property of geodesic
interpolation on any uniquely geodesic space. The mixed-sign
product-manifold case, where the spherical component is \emph{not}
Hadamard and geodesics are not unique, is outside this sketch---
this is the honest boundary of the claim. The identity is cited as
geodesic interpolation in a Hadamard space, not via the hyperbolic
neural-network toolkit of \cite{ganea2018}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the sketch does and does not buy]
It buys: no runaway drift under repeated retrieval (the failure mode of
unbounded Hebbian rules), and a principled step-size schedule. It does
not buy: guarantees under adversarial queries (governance, per
\cite{epistemics}, is still required), or anything about the spherical
component, where geodesics are not unique. We state this plainly because
the literature's habit is to over-claim exactly here.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Forgetting as availability drift}
\label{sec:forgetting}

Wall-clock exponential decay $e^{-\lambda \Delta t}$ is the wrong
retention rule on its own \cite{engramrag}: it evicts foundational facts.
Hand-tuned thresholds are fragile \cite{epistemics}. GyroMem replaces both
with drift of \emph{availability} on the manifold. Each memory carries a
usage potential $u(m,t)$ decaying exponentially in evidence-events
without retrieval and refreshed to $1$ on retrieval. The drift field is
\begin{equation}
\frac{dm}{dt} = -\lambda(u)\,\nabla_m\, \rho(m),
\end{equation}
where $\rho(m)$ is the distance from the region's abstraction centroid
(the Fr\'echet mean of the region's members, computed at dreaming time)
under the product metric, and $\lambda(u)$ is an explicit decreasing
function of $u$, to be fixed in the specification. Low-usage memories drift
\emph{inward}: episodes move toward their summaries, and their
availability for retrieval falls. Drift changes availability only: the
manifold is the index, and raw histories are preserved in a separate
raw-history store, so a drifted memory is re-formed from its preserved
raw history on re-access---that, and only that, is the sense in which
drift is reversible. Drift past the inner horizon---a radius $r_H$ in the
hyperbolic component, to be fixed in the specification---removes the memory
from the retrievable index and schedules it for the deletion operation:
explicit user action recorded in an audit record, removing the record
from the index and the raw-history store. That deletion mechanism is a
requirement, not a built component of this theory paper. This deletion
operation is specified independently of the companion Mnemodynamics
paper's deletion procedure (support-closure removal, per-sentence
receipts, certificate, \S2.5 there): it does not inherit support closure
or receipts, and its probe is the separate probe of H4b below. Composing
the two procedures is future work. Nothing in this
paper should be read as a deletion or retention guarantee: drift is an
availability mechanism, and by design a drifted record remains fully
recoverable until the deletion operation runs. Deployments with
multi-principal or adversarial scope must treat drift as a retention
hazard, not a governance property, and run the security probes of
\S\ref{sec:experiments} before relying on it. On terminology, shared with
the companion Mnemodynamics paper: \emph{relaxation} is loss of
availability; \emph{deletion} is the operation specified above;
\emph{verified} means measured by a stated procedure with a reported
rate, never asserted; \emph{certificate} means the companion paper's
self-issued, unsigned, return-only artifact, not claimed here;
\emph{authority} means the companion paper's (origin, act\_class) pair
---no authority field exists in this paper. This paper's section title uses ``forgetting'' for
availability drift; in user-facing copy, ``forgetting'' means only the
deletion operation.

\subsection{Promotion as radial motion}

The consolidation ladder of \cite{epistemics}---keep / summarize / extract
skill / distill rule / forget---becomes radial position. Dreaming-phase
consolidation is a separate offline procedure, \`a la \cite{engramrag}: it
clusters memories with a clustering algorithm and parameters to be fixed in the
specification, computes each cluster's centroid as the Fr\'echet mean of
its members under the product metric, and moves centroids inward while
pushing members outward slightly (discrimination), i.e., abstraction is
centripetal motion. A ``skill'' is a centroid that has drifted inward past
a radius threshold $r_{\mathrm{skill}}$, to be fixed in the specification, so
that it serves as a retrieval attractor for its former members. Dreaming
is a procedure with parameters, not a consequence of the drift field.
This dreaming clustering is independent of the formation clustering of
\S\ref{sec:formation}: it clusters manifold points under the product
metric, where formation clusters encoder vectors in Euclidean space
before formation; the two procedures share only the name, with separate
algorithms and parameters, both to be fixed in the specification.

\subsection{Unified tier budgeting}
\label{sec:tiers}

KV-cache systems \cite{actkv,agentkv} and external stores
\cite{jevmem,realm,jam} manage disjoint budgets. In GyroMem, the
transient and persistent tiers are \emph{budget partitions over the same
manifold} $\M$: the transient partition is a recency-weighted subset of
the manifold (recent tokens), not a curvature region, and the persistent
partition is the remainder. A single controller allocates a
global memory budget $\mathcal{B}$ across tiers and regions by expected utility per
unit cost, generalizing Jev-Mem's $u_d$ utility estimates \cite{jevmem}:
\begin{equation}
\max_{\{b_r\}} \sum_r U_r(b_r) \quad \text{s.t.}\quad \sum_r b_r \le \mathcal{B},
\end{equation}
where $U_r$ is the estimated marginal retrieval utility of region $r$ at
allocation $b_r$, estimated by Jev-Mem's $u_d$-style estimator as to be stated in
the specification; concavity of $U_r$ is assumed, and the existence of
the maximizer is conditional on that assumption. Eviction in the transient
tier is the same drift dynamics at a faster timescale, with a timescale
parameter to be fixed in the specification; transient content is destroyed on
eviction by a defined destruction step, and no transient record is
treated as persistent. Tier isolation is a requirement: a transient
record must not leak into persistent retrieval, and the ephemeral /
persistent boundary is preserved by construction of the partitions, not
by geometry alone.

\subsection{Just-in-time geodesic research}

Following JAM's refutation of ahead-of-time compression \cite{jam},
GyroMem constructs query-time context by \emph{navigating} $\M$: starting
from the query point formed by the formation rule of
\S\ref{sec:formation}, expand a geodesic ball by radius doubling from an
initial radius $r_0$ (with the candidate set capped at a size to be fixed
in the specification), assess sufficiency---has the Fr\'echet variance of
the ball's members (the mean squared product distance from the ball's
Fr\'echet mean) fallen below $\tau$?---and stop. The threshold $\tau$ is a
tuned parameter, to be fixed in the specification; no claim is made that the
geometric stopping rule replaces hand-tuned thresholds in general. Raw histories
are preserved (JAM's Memorizer lesson); the manifold is the index, not a
compression of it.

\section{Proposed experimental validation}
\label{sec:experiments}

This section pre-registers the empirical program. \emph{No results are
reported}---that is the point. Each hypothesis names its ablation, its
metric, and the condition under which we abandon the corresponding claim.

\textbf{Benchmarks.} LoCoMo and LongMemEval (the field standards; splits
frozen before any run), plus a \emph{transfer benchmark} we will construct
following the Epistemics warning \cite{epistemics}: tasks where
consolidated memory must change what the agent can \emph{do} (skill
transfer, contradiction recovery), scored against a computable oracle,
not an LLM judge alone. The transfer benchmark's construction is specified
here: task families (multi-hop composition, contradiction recovery, skill
reuse across sessions), an executable-check oracle per family, and leakage
controls (held-out task generators; adversarial probing of the benchmark
itself before trusting it). Baselines are Jev-Mem, JAM, and REALM run on
the same harness, plus TIDER's explicit constraint channels
\cite{tider} and the anti-persistence updater \cite{antipersistence} as
named comparators; the encoder is held fixed across the geometry arms of
H1, so geometry is not confounded with encoder or formation.

\textbf{Hypotheses and kill criteria.} All tests use $\alpha = 0.05$ with
paired bootstrap confidence intervals; a win on transfer alone, without
the recall condition where stated, counts as partial support under the
interpretation rule stated per hypothesis, not as a full pass.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=*]
\item[H1 (geometry matters).] Manifold+geodesic retrieval beats the
\emph{identical} system with Euclidean embeddings + cosine on recall@k
and on transfer, at matched total dimension, with the encoder held fixed
across arms. \emph{Metric and margin:} recall@k and transfer score on the
full frozen LoCoMo and LongMemEval splits. \emph{Kill:} no statistically
significant win on either recall or transfer; a win on transfer alone is
partial support (the geometry helps decisions, not retrieval) and triggers
revision of the retrieval claim, not abandonment of the theory; pivot to
Euclidean geodesic updates (still novel vs.\ LLM surgery on cost) or
abandon.
\item[H2 (calibrated formation).] The radial regularizer
(\S\ref{sec:formation}) is necessary: without it, hierarchical
pre-filtering vanishes. \emph{Metric:} pre-filtering precision---the
fraction of retrieved candidates drawn from the query's abstraction
region---with a margin of at least 5 percentage points at $\alpha = 0.05$.
\emph{Kill:} ablated formation matches full formation on this metric;
drop the trained component and simplify the theory.
\item[H3 (cheap consolidation).] Geodesic reconsolidation matches or
exceeds LLM graph surgery (REALM-style) on consolidation quality at
$<\!1\%$ of the update cost. \emph{Metric and margin:} transfer score
within 2 points of REALM-style surgery at $<\!1\%$ of wall-clock and
token cost, where cost includes the confidence ($\kappa$) computation and
encoder training amortized over the benchmark, plus ANN rerank cost.
\emph{Kill:} quality gap over 2 points, or cost at or over 1\%;
retain geometry for retrieval only.
\item[H4a (availability).] Geometric drift preserves availability of
load-bearing facts under budget pressure, with recovery on re-access.
\emph{Metric:} at least 99\% recovery of load-bearing facts on re-access
after budget pressure (tolerance: at most 1\% irreversible availability
loss). \emph{Kill:} recovery below 99\%; the drift field is wrong and must
be redesigned.
\item[H4b (deletion).] The deletion operation of
\S\ref{sec:forgetting} removes the record from the retrievable index and
the raw-history store, with the residual recovery rate reported by a
probe whose reader, channels, and thresholds are to be fixed in the
specification before any run. \emph{Kill:} the deletion operation fails to remove from
index and raw store, or the residual rate is not reported.
\item[H5 (unified budget).] Joint tier budgeting beats separate
KV-cache + external-store management on end-to-end task throughput at
fixed total memory. \emph{Metric and margin:} tasks per hour at a fixed
quality threshold, plus latency, with a margin of at least 5\% at
$\alpha = 0.05$. \emph{Kill:} no win at the stated margin; keep the tiers
separate and the manifold for the external store only.
\end{description}

\textbf{Security probes (scope conditions, not guarantees).} Three probes
run before any multi-principal or adversarial deployment claim: a steering
probe (can repeated queries drag a memory toward the query?---the
contraction mechanism of \S\ref{sec:reconsolidation} is attacker-steerable
by construction under adversarial queries); a deletion-residual probe
(does a drifted record remain recoverable, and does the deletion operation
remove it from every channel?); and a cross-principal leakage probe (does
one principal's record influence another's retrieval?). Each probe has a
pre-stated kill: steering, residual recovery, or leakage above the
Phase-0 thresholds blocks the corresponding deployment claim. The theory
as stated is single-principal and non-adversarial in scope; governance
under adversarial queries is still required \cite{epistemics}.

\textbf{Methodological commitments.} Fixed random seeds; paired bootstrap
confidence intervals; ablations that change exactly one component; public
release of code, configs, and the transfer benchmark; independent
replication invited before any product claims. We will publish negative
results with the same prominence as positive ones.

\section{Discussion: where this is most likely to fail}
\label{sec:failure}

\textbf{1. The hierarchy assumption.} Hyperbolic geometry wins when data
is hierarchical \cite{nickel2017}. Agent memories may be messier---flat
lists of facts, adversarial noise, contradictory updates---in which case
the manifold is expensive ornament. H1 is designed to detect exactly this.

\textbf{2. ANN immaturity.} Product-manifold nearest-neighbor search lacks
FAISS-grade tooling. Our fallback stack---tangent-space candidate
generation at the query point plus exact geodesic rerank---is $O(\log n)$
for candidates but exact rerank is linear in the capped candidate set; at
billion-scale this may lose to Euclidean HNSW on latency. This is an
engineering risk, not a theoretical one, and we carry it openly.

\textbf{3. The spherical component.} Non-unique geodesics, no contraction
guarantee, curvature-sign mixing without a full theory (noted in the
Proposition's proof sketch). If the spherical component causes more
trouble than value, the honest move is a hyperbolic--Euclidean product,
and we have pre-committed to reporting that. The fallback is
pre-committed here: if spherical-component ablations show no win, the
theory drops the spherical component rather than retaining it.

\textbf{4. Encoder calibration.} H2's radial regularizer is the theory's
one trained component and therefore its most fragile: if abstraction
level cannot be calibrated into embedding norm, formation collapses to
an arbitrary projection and the hierarchy story fails.

\textbf{5. Evaluation gaming.} Beating LoCoMo is not the goal; transfer
is. If our transfer benchmark turns out to be gameable or circular, H1--H5
prove nothing. We will adversarially probe our own benchmark before
trusting it, following the anti-leakage discipline of \cite{epistemics}.

\textbf{6. Stronger null hypotheses.} The fairest baselines are not
mem0-style systems but the best 2026 systems (Jev-Mem, JAM, REALM) with
\emph{our} evaluation harness. A win against weak baselines is not a win.

\textbf{7. Prior art may narrow the contribution.} No verified
product-manifold agent-memory system was found in the surveyed
literature (see \S\ref{sec:nearest}); if one is demonstrated under
independent evaluation, GyroMem's novelty narrows to formalization, the
three mechanisms, and the evaluation program. We would welcome that
result---it would confirm the geometry bet while disciplining our claims.

\textbf{8. Retention and security scope.} Drift is an availability
mechanism, not a deletion mechanism: a drifted record remains recoverable
until the deletion operation runs, the manifold carries no origin, owner,
or authority information, and reconsolidation is steerable by repeated
queries. Any deployment beyond the single-principal, non-adversarial
scope of this paper must run the security probes of
\S\ref{sec:experiments} and treat drift as a retention hazard.

\section{From theory to system: engineering roadmap}

Briefly, since this paper's job is the theory: (0) prior-art sweep and
formal spec---this paper; (1) Python prototype (Poincar\'e/Lorentz store,
gyrovector ops, drift dynamics) on a LoCoMo subset; (2) full evaluation
per \S\ref{sec:experiments} with kill-criteria gates; (3) if gates pass,
open-source engine, public benchmark release, hosted API. Each phase is
gated; phases do not proceed on hope.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

GyroMem reframes agentic memory geometrically: a product manifold with
per-region curvature to be fixed in the specification, geodesic retrieval,
reconsolidation as geodesic transport, forgetting as availability drift,
and one budget across tiers.
It is motivated by four gaps stated in the recent literature, grounded
in established mathematics, and---unusually for a theory paper---shipped
with its own falsification program. It may be wrong; \S\ref{sec:failure}
explains exactly how. If it is right, memory stops being a pile of
vectors with heuristics on top and becomes what it should have been all
along: a space with a shape, in which remembering is moving, learning is
transport, and forgetting is drift of availability that can be undone
before deletion.

\section{Gyrovector formulary}
\label{app:formulary}

We follow the convention of \cite{ganea2018}: the Poincar\'e ball
$\B^n_c = \{x \in \R^n : c\|x\|^2 < 1\}$ has curvature $-c < 0$.
M\"obius addition is
\begin{equation}
x \oplus_c y =
\frac{(1 + 2c\langle x,y\rangle + c\|y\|^2)\,x + (1 - c\|x\|^2)\,y}
{1 + 2c\langle x,y\rangle + c^2\|x\|^2\|y\|^2}.
\end{equation}
M\"obius scalar multiplication by $r \in \R$:
\begin{equation}
r \otimes_c x = \tanh\!\big(r\,\operatorname{artanh}(\sqrt{c}\|x\|)\big)\,
\frac{x}{\sqrt{c}\|x\|}.
\end{equation}
The induced distance is
\begin{equation}
d_c(x,y) = \frac{2}{\sqrt{c}}\,
\operatorname{artanh}\!\big(\sqrt{c}\,\|{-x} \oplus_c y\|\big),
\end{equation}
equivalently $d_c(x,y) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\operatorname{arcosh}\!\left(1 +
\frac{2c\|x-y\|^2}{(1-c\|x\|^2)(1-c\|y\|^2)}\right)$.
The exponential and logarithmic maps at $x$ are
\begin{equation}
\exp^c_x(v) = x \oplus_c \left(
\tanh\!\left(\sqrt{c}\,\frac{\lambda^c_x \|v\|}{2}\right)
\frac{v}{\sqrt{c}\,\|v\|}\right), \qquad
\lambda^c_x = \frac{2}{1 - c\|x\|^2},
\end{equation}
with $\log^c_x$ its inverse. For numerical work near the boundary we
recommend the Lorentz (hyperboloid) model, which is isometric and avoids
the $\|x\| \to 1/\sqrt{c}$ instability.
