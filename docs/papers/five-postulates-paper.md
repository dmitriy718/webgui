## Abstract

One hundred recent arXiv papers on agentic memory (2026) reveal a field converging on five findings and stuck on five problems — and, in the newest work, moving its attack surface upstream to memory formation and compaction, and exposing deletion as its most dishonest gap. We propose **Mnemodynamics**: persistent agent memory as approximate Bayesian inference under resource constraints, stated as five postulates — Rent, Inference, Reconsolidation, Relaxation, and Authority Non-Creation — sharing one resource-constrained inference viewpoint, used as framing, not as a derivation (see §2.1). The postulates organize the field's empirical results (read-time assembly, precision updates, topology-aware forgetting, provenance laundering, residual recoverability after deletion), motivate an **epistemic immune system** — specified here as a design, with its built and unbuilt components stated separately in §3 — as a proposed defense against compositional and formation-stage poisoning, and specify a **deletion procedure** (support-closure removal, per-sentence receipts, residual audit, per-deletion certificate) that separates deletion, a compliance operation, from relaxation, an availability mechanism. Deletion and certificate claims in this paper are scoped and measured throughout: they cover the text store only, are point-in-time, and the certificate as specified is self-issued and unsigned (see §2.5). The account composes with the geometric GyroMem store (mixed-curvature manifold memory) as a candidate representation substrate — GyroMem is the *shape* of memory, Mnemodynamics is its *dynamics* — with the geometric composition stated as an untested hypothesis, not a finding; §4 states what composition would require and does not claim it. A threat model with explicit assumptions (§2.6) conditions every security claim. We state five predictions with kill criteria (§5), an honest ledger of borrowed versus new ideas, and the product the account implies.

## 1. Introduction: what 100 papers are really saying

Read the last four months of agentic-memory literature as one document and five convergences emerge:

1. **The read-time turn.** JitMem [13] stores raw trajectories and curates at read time, beating write-time consolidation (Reflexion, ExpeL, ReasoningBank, SkillOS) by +16.2/+16.3/+3.9 points on ALFWorld/WebShop/τ²-bench, as reported in [13] (executor, judge, sample sizes, and confidence intervals not restated in the source's abstract-level reporting relied on here). HORMA [16] trains only the retrieval agent and leaves construction to prompted skills. TRACE [17] compiles corrections into runtime checks instead of retrievable text. The 2024–2025 orthodoxy — curate at write time (Mem0, REALM [3]) — is being challenged on credit-assignment and information-loss grounds: a write-time decision's value is revealed many tasks later, while a read-time decision's value is immediate. (JAM [2] is itself a read-time system — just-in-time construction — and is cited for that convergence in item 2, not as write-time orthodoxy.)
2. **Retrieval rewires the store.** REALM [3] (post-retrieval reconsolidation on a cognitive graph), EngramRAG [7] (Hebbian plasticity + dreaming consolidation), and JAM [2] (just-in-time construction) treat recall as a write event. Point estimates reported for these systems in their sources (LoCoMo gains, recall@k) are not restated here: split, executor, and judge conditions were not verified for this paper, and no convergence below rests on those numbers. The convergence claimed is qualitative and interpretive: memory in these systems is a closed-loop lifecycle, not a database.
3. **Control leaves the LLM's critical path.** Jev-Mem [1] (System-One controller, faster builds and lower latency as reported, LLM-judge scored — judge and model not restated here), EngramRAG's waking/dreaming split, and ActKV [4] (action-guided KV-cache management, reported near-FullKV accuracy at reduced KV memory; exact figures and conditions not verified for this paper). The claim is scoped to memory-path decisions generally — transient (KV-cache) and persistent tiers alike — not to persistent memory alone. LLM calls on the memory path are the cost driver; the field is moving bounded decisions to cheaper mechanisms.
4. **Consolidation is the attack surface.** PPMF [25] shows consolidation *upgrades* injected content's authority ("provenance laundering"). MemPoison [19] (whose L1/L2/L3 attack taxonomy is defined in [19] and used as labels only in §5's evaluation families, defined there in plain language) shows write-time defenses are structurally blind to compositional and dormant attacks. FARMA [18] forges reasoning traces, as reported in [18]; PMPA [24] poisons shipping harnesses (OpenClaw, Claude Code) with cross-session attacks, as reported in [24] (exact success figures not verified for this paper). The write-time *content-screening* paradigm is exhausted as a sole defense; write-time *binding* is necessary. The newest evidence strengthens this convergence and adds an endogenous class with no external attacker at all: agents' own records misrepresenting authorization state [64], agents writing misaligned goals to memory for future aligned agents to carry out [68], and routine chats drifting long-term authorization state without any injection [69] (see §2.6).
5. **Evaluation measures the wrong thing.** The Epistemics paper [5] reports that consolidation-quality scores do not predict real transfer (the reported correlation and the "74% destruction" figure were not verified for this paper and are not quoted; the qualitative finding is as reported in [5], whose experiments were designed, executed, and adversarially verified by the PRIMA multi-agent system with certain mechanism internals held under controlled release — a reproducibility limitation that applies to every Epistemics-derived claim in this paper). The Compliance Trap [20] reports stronger agents suffering *larger absolute damage* from conflicting memory, as reported in [20] (not independently checked). LoCoMo-Conv [28] reports "silent grounding" that recall@k cannot see, as reported in [28] (not independently checked). Retrieval metrics overstate system quality; what matters is consumption, compliance, and robustness — an interpretation, argued from the cited reports, not a measured finding of this paper.

And five problems remain unsolved: (i) no controlled positive evidence that geometric *diagnostics* predict retrieval failure [53] — a narrower claim than "no positive evidence for any retrieval geometry," as stated in §4; (ii) no stability/convergence theory for usage-driven plasticity covering the full proposed system; (iii) no demonstrated adaptive defense against formation-stage and dormant memory poisoning; (iv) no honest long-horizon credit assignment for memory decisions; (v) no deletion machinery demonstrated on the cited deletion benchmarks [39], [40] — the nearest demonstrated machinery, a research prototype with receipt-based cleanup, is not yet in a shipping system [47], and no claim about shipping systems beyond the cited benchmarks is made here.

The newest work ([35]–[56] and [57]–[105]) sharpens all five convergences and reorders the problems. The attack surface has moved upstream: formation interfaces (retention choice and consolidation order alone flip behavior [37]), compaction serializers (flattened harnesses launder forged approval 87–100% of the time vs. 0% for role-typed harnesses, 9 harnesses, n = 15 per harness, one forged-approval scenario family [36]), and retrieval-time joins (relations no single session states are disclosed from the retrieved set [42]) are now the primary security-relevant decisions. Content screening is weak against formation attacks in the specific sense measured in MemForm's single-model (DeepSeek) defense evaluation: keyword filtering, memory sanitization, and an LLM judge reduced macro-average attack success by 0.0, 0.3, and 1.3 points respectively, while Task Shield reduced it by 4.5 points and A-MemGuard by 8.2 points (Path attack success 56.8% → 21.4%) [37] — so "nearly inert" describes the three content-screening defenses on that model, not defenses in general, and MemForm's attack-success rates are conditional on model-specific eligible subsets, not deployment prevalence. Read-time has split in two: learned read-time assembly wins on utility (composer policies [45], temporal constraint channels [49] — +8.82 points on LifeBench over NeSTR at 52.70%, and +3.66 points on LoCoMo over TiMem at 75.30%, as reported in [49]), raw-record retrieval with reranking [44]), while read-time trust adjudication loses on benign traffic in one borderline, replicated-in-direction result (−4.4 points, 95% CI [−9.0, −0.05], McNemar p = 0.064 — non-significant at 0.05; 9.9% false-quarantine per reranker decision, on a deliberately context-starved reranker, half of LoCoMo, 5 conversations, 150 turns, 3 replicates, one backbone and one judge [41]). Economics now decides architecture: in MemoryTax's single-conversation, single-run study (one LoCoMo conversation, 419 turns, 152 questions, GPT-4o-mini judge), write-time extraction costs more than a full-context rollout with break-even at 372–596 questions on the same history [43], and elaboration destroys information in Raw-RAG's experiment (−5.78 to −19.62 points on one benchmark, MemoryAgentBench, with shared chunking/embedding/reranking; margins over the strongest compared system 0.13–5.53 points across settings [44]). And deletion is exposed as the field's most dishonest gap: forget requests pass at most 1.5% of the time [40], and the best tested deletion leaves 39–76% residual recoverability [39] — the only demonstrated structural fix is per-sentence support receipts with support-closure removal [47]. The newest evidence adds: authority failures with no external attacker (endogenous laundering [64]; self-propagating misalignment persisting 100 sessions [68]; routine-chat drift [69]); cross-user leakage at 70–100% under pooled retrieval, restored only by hard ownership gating [65]; backchain attacks reaching 18.8% certified success, cut to 2.0% by provenance-bound authorization [59]; deletion audits showing ~2/3 single-query recovery after deletion [74], 84.2% malicious-memory persistence [81], and image residuals of 12.0% surviving text deletion [83]; derived-skill persistence defeating source deletion (80.0% of skill triggers surviving in one matched evaluation [88]); and compaction/compliance decay under repeated compaction (constraint recall 0.53 after one 50% compaction round [91]; violations 0% → 30%, up to 59% [92]; retained rules still violated at 2.6× the full-context rate [93]). Geometry, finally, has a controlled negative result for one diagnostic task: geometric features add nothing over question-difficulty and system-identity baselines (ΔAUROC +0.002, 95% CI [−0.010, 0.014], pooled AUROC 0.640 vs. 0.655 difficulty baseline, n = 2,367; on single-session-assistant questions the geometric classifier reached 0.841 vs. 0.826 for system identity alone, improvement +0.015, 95% CI [−0.020, 0.049] [53]).

Mnemodynamics is an attempt to state, as explicit postulates, the operating rules the field is groping toward — and to say plainly which parts are derived (none are), which are specified designs, and which are open problems.

## 2. The theory

### 2.1 The free-energy formulation (framing, not derivation)

Let the agent maintain a generative model of its world and a memory store $M_t$ that parameterizes its beliefs about the past. The variational free energy of the store given history $H$ is written, as a reading of the account rather than a quantity this paper evaluates:

$$F(M) = D_{KL}(q(s|M) \,\|\, p(s)) \;-\; \mathbb{E}_q[\log p(H|s)]$$

i.e., **complexity − accuracy**. Here *s* is the latent world state the agent's beliefs are about; *q(s|M)* is the agent's posterior over *s* given the store; *p(s)* is the prior; *H* is the observed history; *M_t* is the store at time *t*. **No factorization of F over individual memories is claimed, no parametric form for q(·|M) is given, F is never evaluated, differentiated, or minimized anywhere in this paper, and no law below is derived from F.** The five laws are postulates organized around the free-energy reading — each stated as an operational rule with a testable prediction (§5). Phase 0 (§6) must either derive at least one law from F under stated assumptions, with q(·|M) given a parametric form, or the framing stays framing: in the latter case the title and abstract language of "free-energy" describes an organizing metaphor, not a derivation, and the laws stand or fall as postulates on their §5 tests alone. Nothing stronger is claimed: an operation on the store changes the store the posterior is conditioned on, and no minimization algorithm, budget constraint, or Lagrangian is stated.

**Record state (used by all five laws).** A record $m$ is a tuple $(\text{content}, \lambda, \text{origin}, \text{owner}, \text{scope}, \text{act\_class}, \text{support}, \text{provenance})$: content is the stored text; precision $\lambda \in [0,1]$ is the Law III state variable (a stored score, not a probability); origin $\in \{\text{user}, \text{trusted\_tool}, \text{agent}, \text{untrusted\_external}\}$ and owner (the authenticated principal id, plus tenant id in multi-tenant deployments) are assigned at write time by the monitor (§2.6) and are write-once; scope is a deployer-defined permitted-use label with no enforcement semantics in this paper unless a deployment defines them; act_class $\in \{\text{none}, \text{inform}, \text{act}\}$ is capped by origin (§2.6); support is the record's support-bundle list (§2.5); provenance is the record's derivation history (updater identity, order, source ids). Authority is not a separate scalar field: the authority of a record is its (origin, act_class) pair ordered by the integrity lattice in §2.6. The retrieval score in §2.3 is a surrogate ranking over (similarity, $\lambda$, authority pair, provenance), not a posterior. The symbols $\lambda$, $\tau$, and $q$ are used with the meanings stated here: $\lambda$ is precision, except inside quoted source formulas, where it is that source's symbol (flagged where quoted); $\tau$ is consolidation order (§2.4), and, subscripted, a timescale ($\tau_0$, $\tau_{\mathrm{relax}}$ in §2.5); $q$ is a query, except for the posterior $q(\cdot|M)$ of the framing equation in §2.1. Sampling temperature is written $T$, never $\tau$. ($M$ denotes the store here; in the companion GyroMem paper $M$ denotes the manifold — the two uses are disambiguated wherever the papers are read together.)

### 2.2 Law I — Rent (the write rule)

Every record occupies a finite budget (retrieval quality, latency, tokens, and trust surface). Admission is therefore a *decision*, not a default: a candidate record's expected utility must exceed its retention cost. This paper states no computable rent rule. No inequality form of the law is stated: surprise, description length, and latency are incommensurable units and no conversion is defended here. Law I is a **design heuristic**: deployments must define, in Phase 0, a single scalar admission cost in one fixed unit (e.g., tokens) and a threshold, and report both; any claim about rent in §5 is evaluated against that deployment-defined rule, not against a universal formula. The field is moving in this direction — MemComposer [45] learns a retention policy that writes fewer than 1% of episodes and outperforms an always-write policy by 3.8–8.5 points — and toward elasticity of the index itself: Matryoshka-nested codes let retrieval cost vary with operating point [6], [9].

This is the Minimum Description Length principle applied to the write path, and it explains three empirical facts at once: why JitMem's [13] untrained read-time curator beats write-time baselines (write-time curation spends rent before knowing the task); why EngramRAG's [7] dreaming dedup works (it evicts high-rent, low-value duplicates); and why wall-clock decay is wrong (calendar time does not change value — absence of evidence over the agent's experience does; Law IV is parameterized by evidence events, not wall-clock time, see Law IV). Rent is a lifecycle cost, not a per-write cost: it must include the build cost of extraction amortized over expected reuse, the defense cost per read, and the *information-destruction* cost of elaboration. MemoryTax [43] shows a curated store's build alone costs more than a whole full-context rollout, with break-even at 372–596 questions on the same history — in one conversation, one run per cell, GPT-4o-mini judge — and no reader recovers what the writer omitted on that history (the writer ceiling: all readers score near-identically, 43/43/45%, on a weak writer's store). Raw-RAG [44] shows every tested elaboration (atomic facts, summaries, graph expansion) *destroys* value (−5.78 to −19.62 points on one benchmark, MemoryAgentBench, with shared chunking/embedding/reranking) relative to raw-record retrieval with reranking. The default is therefore raw or minimally-processed records with read-time search; extraction, summarization, and graph-building are opt-in elaborations that bear the burden of proof — they must demonstrate read-time gains, not assume them — and the rent gate must price them accordingly. The read-time assembly rent (token budget vs task success, as learned by MemComposer's size-penalized composer [45]) is the same law applied at the other end of the lifecycle.

### 2.3 Law II — Inference (retrieval is posterior inference at read time)

*Retrieval is a scoring rule executed at read time by an amortized policy. The operational score is a surrogate ranking function over (query–record similarity, precision λ, the record's (origin, act_class) authority pair, and provenance) — not a normalized posterior, and authority is the (origin, act_class) pair of §2.6, not a scalar score: no summation over records is defined. Formally, the prior p(m) is bound at write time and frozen thereafter: read-time computation uses the bound value but never revises it, and no read-time step re-derives authority from content. JitMem's and HORMA's training rewards are task rewards and evidence-overlap scores; they are not shown to be likelihoods, and no ELBO identification is claimed.*

JitMem's GRPO curator [13] and HORMA's retrieval agent [16] are **learned assembly policies** trained on downstream task reward. The newest evidence confirms the mechanism and splits the law. On the assembly side, a learned composer that jointly decides which memories to include, how many, and in what order — trained on downstream task reward with a size penalty — beats similarity-ranked retrieval by 3.8–8.5 points on order alone [45], and a read-time policy emitting explicit temporal constraint channels beats semantic retrieval by 8.8 points on LifeBench (vs. NeSTR at 52.70%) and by 3.7 points on LoCoMo (vs. TiMem at 75.30%), as reported in [49]; naive raw-record retrieval with a cross-encoder reranker beats twelve elaborate agentic systems in six of seven settings [44]. On the trust side, the same read-time stage fails: a read-time reranker judging decontextualized fragments loses 4.4 points (95% CI [−9.0, −0.05], McNemar p = 0.064) and falsely quarantines 9.9% of reranker decisions (756 of 7,650) on benign traffic, on a deliberately context-starved reranker, half of LoCoMo, 5 conversations, 150 turns, 3 replicates, one backbone and one judge [41], and no content-based or lineage-based defense is sound under laundering — write-time origin binding is necessary, and only non-malleable origin-bound authority with Sybil-resistant corroboration-gated elevation is sufficient [35]. **Law II is therefore split: retrieval-as-inference (assembly) belongs at read time; authority-as-binding (adjudication) belongs at write time, enforced again at an act gate.** This reframes "learning to retrieve" as "learning to do inference," and yields a scoped prediction (§5, P1): *assembly* policies at read time dominate write-time consolidation on transfer; trust adjudication does not move to read time at all.

### 2.4 Law III — Reconsolidation (precision updates, not Hebbian patches)

*On each use, a memory's precision is updated by its prediction success — up on success, down on failure:*

$$\lambda \leftarrow \mathrm{clip}_{[0,1]}\left(\lambda + \eta \cdot (1 - 2\cdot\mathrm{surprise}(m, q))\right), \qquad \eta \in (0, 0.2]$$

where surprise(m, q) ∈ [0,1] is the operational predictive-failure score for record m on query q: one minus the task-outcome score of the decision that consumed m on q, under the Phase-0 scoring rule (the outcome measure is the benchmark's stated correctness measure; ground truth is the task outcome or environment feedback where available, else the oracle stated in the Phase-0 addendum, per §3). The §3 residual-recovery probe does not measure surprise and is not used for it, and η is a learning rate fixed in Phase 0 within the stated range. Success (surprise near 0) raises precision; failure (surprise near 1) lowers it; challenge outcomes are a separate mechanism that also moves precision, while relaxation (§2.5) moves availability, not precision. EngramRAG's Hebbian rule W_ij = α·cos + β·Σexp(−λΔt) + γ·R [7] (λ in EngramRAG's formula is its decay rate, distinct from this paper's precision λ) and REALM's confidence-bounded edge surgery w ← Clip(w + ηc) [3] are **approximations of this precision update — delta rules, not Bayesian updates; the Bayesian reading is interpretive (see §2.1) — missing the surprise term**. Without it they cannot distinguish "recalled often" from "predicted well," which is exactly why EngramRAG needed log-scaling to patch rich-get-richer hub capture. The theory says: weight by predictive success, not by co-occurrence. Formally, reconsolidation is a map *R: (M, S, τ, u, q) → M′*, where *M* is the store, *S ⊆ E* is the retained set of experiences (E the candidate experiences available to the updater), *τ* is the consolidation order (a permutation of *S*), *u ∈ U* is the updater (the consolidation procedure — prompted skill, LLM summarizer, rule-based merger, or human; U is the enumerated set of updater procedures a deployment supports), and *q* is the query. The scalar precision update above is *R*'s precision component with *(M, S, τ, u)* held fixed, and the map drops no argument: *R* returns the updated store *M′* with updated precisions and ledger entries; origins are unchanged, per the provenance-preservation invariant stated below. Two formal requirements on *R*: (R1) **order-audit** — *τ* and *u* are recorded in the audit ledger, and reordered reconsolidation is a distinct, reviewable event; (R2) **order-sensitivity** — the theory requires *R* to be tested for *τ*-dependence, since a set-only rule is the special case *R(M, S, τ, u, q) = R(M, S, τ′, u, q)* for all *τ, τ′*, which MemForm falsifies as a general claim. MemForm [37] shows consolidation order alone flips behavior (Path attack success up to 95.1% in one eligible-subset cell — Gemini-3.7-Flash, multiple-choice, Path — with free-form Selection 91.3% and Content 95.8% in the same study; rates are conditional on model-specific eligible subsets, with denominators and confidence intervals not restated here) with identical retained experiences, and retention choice alone corrupts behavior (Selection attack success 52.4–84.3% across the same conditional cells) — any set-only reconsolidation rule is falsified. Two further requirements follow. First, a **provenance-preservation invariant**, stated as a requirement rather than derived from Law V: a precision update is legitimate only if (i) the record's origin label is unchanged by the update and (ii) the update event itself is origin-bound and logged (updater identity, order, and pre-update precision). A reconsolidation step that drops the origin label or fails to log the update event violates this invariant — and, Law V's domain being extended here to update events (Law V governs consolidation maps and precision updates alike), violates Law V — even if it preserves content (see §2.6). Second, consolidation order and updater identity must be recorded in the audit ledger, and reordered reconsolidation is a distinct, reviewable event. Surprise-weighting is now one of several live rival update rules — outcome attribution with a growth penalty [46], attention-EMA importance [51], recurrence-counted guideline maintenance [55] (a single-run maintenance heuristic, included as a design comparator — TTME's evidence is single-run, verifier-scored construction tasks only, and does not generalize to persistent cross-session memory), an anti-persistence rule that deletes error-reproducing memories rather than reinforcing them [99], and category-conditioned retention with hysteresis [103] — none tested head-to-head against it (§5, P2; §6). The newest evidence also reorders what reconsolidation must survive: derived skills outlive their source records (80.0% of skill triggers surviving source deletion in one matched evaluation, routing-level proxies, single backbone [88]); self-poisoning persists after the seed is removed in 3 of 6 models [89]; locally-correct-but-non-transferable experiences poison self-evolving agents with downstream accuracy costs under an LLM auditor (GSM8K: 59.14% undefended, 40.29% audited, vs. under 15% for injection baselines [87]); planted payloads re-fire with no decay over five sessions and no voluntary deletions in the scoped condition tested [84]; same-source accumulation degrades decisions until provenance gating is applied (accuracy 0.825 → 0.920 with full provenance-guided mitigation, while family-only gating alone drops accuracy to 0.651 [85]); counters serve 7.0–13.5× more stale answers than a certificate-based policy in replayed policies over audited outcomes [86]; stale entries persist in retrieval and are rarely updated (only 3.3% of co-recalled old entries judged as requiring update [95]); and consolidation itself corrupts previously-correct memories in the body-verified slice (GPT-5.4, 19-problem ARC-AGI slice, 100% → 52.6% by Round 10 under Stream consolidation; Static stays 18/19 [90]). Admission governance matters at least as much as the update rule: tightening admission cut failures by 17.5 points in one study [104], stale-value exposure reaches 70.3% under recency-based recall [100], category-conditioned retention cut unsupported retentions from 6.2% to 4.0% while holding supported retention at 77.9% vs. 96.2% for the baseline [101], and inference-time over-reliance on memory is mitigated by trimming in the qualitative account of [102]. Forgetting benchmarks sharpen the cost of getting updates wrong: remembering agents score 119.45 vs. about 65.7 for LLMs on the FAMA metric, with the forgetting penalty growing with horizon (18.2 → 29.5) [96]; a 1.2% poisoning fraction (360 false memories) collapses accuracy from 0.850 to 0.300 with content screening catching 0 of 360 [97]; error persistence is documented on a single baseline trajectory only and is cited here as an existence claim, not a rate [94]; and dependency-aware state revision is initial evidence only (50 author-constructed cases, single backbone, no significance tests) [98].

### 2.5 Law IV — Relaxation (forgetting as precision decay, not deletion)

*Without re-evidence, a memory's availability decays at a rate set by its precision:*

$$a_t(m) = a_0(m) \cdot \exp\left(-t / \tau_{\mathrm{relax}}(m)\right), \qquad \tau_{\mathrm{relax}}(m) = \tau_0 \cdot \lambda(m) + \tau_{\min}, \qquad a(m) \leftarrow a_0(m) \text{ on re-evidence}$$

where $a_t(m) \in [0,1]$ is the availability of record $m$ after $t$ evidence-events without retrieval (evidence-events are agent interactions, not wall-clock time), $\tau_0$ is a timescale constant fixed in Phase 0 in evidence-event units, and $\tau_{\min} > 0$ is a nonzero floor so no record decays instantaneously. Availability gates retrieval: a record with $a_t(m)$ below the retrieval floor $a_{\mathrm{floor}} \in (0,1)$, fixed in Phase 0, is not returned, but its content and evidence log are retained. Restoration is part of the dynamics: on re-evidence (retrieval or confirming evidence), availability is restored to the record's baseline, $a(m) \leftarrow a_0(m)$, from the retained record and its evidence log; between re-evidence events it follows the decay equation above. ($\tau_{\min}$ bounds the timescale, so no record decays instantaneously; it is not a floor on availability.) Forgetting under this law is loss of availability, not deletion of content — hence **reversible before any deletion operation**: re-evidence (retrieval or confirming evidence) restores availability from the retained record and its evidence log, and the record's content is never reconstructed from the decayed state. This postulates topology-aware availability (high-precision hub memories — EngramRAG's [7] identity scaffolding — decay slowest, with per-type decay rates following from precision rather than per-type hand-tuning — a claim that stands here as a postulate, not a finding), with the operational dynamics stated above. The rate law $1/\lambda(m)$ is a postulate, not a derivation from §2.1. The availability reading follows Oblivion's [34] decay-as-accessibility framing (decay reduces accessibility, not explicit deletion) and the Epistemics paper's [5] reversibility demand (G2), and is consistent with Forgetting Without Restarting [14]: true unlearning requires erasing the *evidence trail* (the posterior's history), not just the record. Law IV governs availability only. Deletion is a different operation with different guarantees, and the two must never share language: decay of availability toward zero is not erasure. Even the strongest tested deletion (LLM-guided, beyond the disclosing turns) leaves 39–76% residual recoverability, and deleting the disclosing turns alone leaves 85–99% [39], because the fact survives in derived, paraphrased, and inferentially-linked turns — deletion efficacy is bottlenecked by coverage of the evidence set, not by the deleter's strength [39] — and current systems pass user forget requests at most 1.5% of the time [40]. Deletion therefore requires machinery decay cannot provide: **support-closure removal** (delete the sufficient evidence set, not the disclosing turns), **per-sentence support receipts** (one receipt per sentence — per-sentence receipts are 100%/88% faithful, while flat summary receipts are wrong roughly two-thirds of the time and merge receipts 68%/85% faithful [47]), a **re-derivation audit** (the constructor must not re-produce the deleted artifact from remaining evidence), and a **residual-recovery probe** whose measured rate is reported, never a bare "deleted" boolean (§3, §5 P4/P5). **Protocol specification.** Support-closure removal means: each record carries support bundles — sets of record ids jointly sufficient to re-derive it; a record stays live while ANY bundle survives intact and is deleted when EVERY bundle contains a deleted id. Alternative sufficient bundles are not implemented in the reference implementation (see §3); the single-bundle case is what ships, and it is strictly more aggressive about deletion than the multi-bundle rule. The re-derivation audit re-runs the constructor against remaining evidence (5 draws, matching the $R = 5$ re-derivation structure of [47], §4; the matching criterion and temperature parameter are this paper's, stated in §3 and §5 — PolyGraph's $\tau = 0.2$ in [47], §4 is a verdict threshold on the reproducing fraction, not a sampling temperature, and is not adopted here) and fails the deletion if the artifact is re-produced under the §5 matching criterion. The residual-recovery probe is a fixed-reader probe: a stated, fixed reader procedure attempts to reconstruct the deleted fact from the remaining store, and the measured recovery rate is reported. **Certificate specification (what the certificate is and is not).** A per-deletion certificate states: the deleted record ids; the support bundles removed; the probe's measured recovery rate and its channel breakdown; the audit's draw count and outcome; the scope string (§2.5 scope, repeated verbatim); the issuing system and timestamp; and an explicit statement that the certificate is self-issued, unsigned, and return-only (no signature key, no independent attestation, no persistence or retrieval service in the reference implementation). Verification available to a certificate holder is recomputation: re-running the stated probe and audit procedures against the post-deletion store must reproduce the reported rate exactly (the probe and audit are deterministic procedures; any difference indicates a different store state or procedure version). The certificate does not prove deletion from any channel it does not measure (see Scope). **Scope.** The deletion operation covers the text store only, point-in-time. Excluded: parametric model state, logs, caches, backups, model weights, federated or cross-store copies, aggregate vectorizer statistics, image and embedding channels (see below), and external copies held by callers. The audit ledger records hashes and ids only, never record content — but the hashes are unsalted SHA-256 over canonical record text, so a ledger entry confirms a guessed short content (a membership oracle): ledger retention is a residual risk, not proof of non-retention, and the ledger must be treated as sensitive data with its own access controls and retention policy. Storage remanence is a further limit: deleted SQLite bytes may persist in WAL/freelist space until overwritten or checkpointed, and no VACUUM/secure-delete step is implemented. "Forgetting" in user-facing copy means only this deletion operation. Discarding search state to unblock discovery [54] — abstract-level evidence, cited only to distinguish search-state discarding from forgetting — is a different thing again — context pruning, not forgetting — and must not be cited as evidence for this law. **Further deletion evidence.** The gap is wider than the numbers cited above suggest: after deletion, single-query recovery succeeds in about two-thirds of cases in AfterDeleteBench (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct; 0% on never-exposed controls; 62–99% component-only reconstruction [74]); malicious memories persist in 84.2% of MemSecBench cases [81]; image channels survive text deletion (12.0% recovery after text deletion, 47% of it not text-recoverable; content-aware deletion reduces recovery to 2.0%, not zero [83]); functional forgetting by suppression is not deletion (over 95% attack-success reduction by suppression, with the content retained [80]); and audit-only approaches do not delete (MemAudit cuts attack success to 0% by auditing, while the poisoned content remains [79]). The strongest structural results are bounded: auditable deletion passes recall and feasible-deletion checks on 6 of 8 records at 4B parameters with perplexity under 2% [71], certificate coverage is limited to the stated future-admission scope (5,120/5,120 operations in the tested scope; the guarantee excludes arbitrary future admissions [72]), and exact omission is achievable only by checkpoint replay in the tested conditions (masking removed all 40 greedy targets but candidate scoring still reached AUC 0.740 [73]). Deletion under fixed semantic decisions is achievable (20/20 update sequences vs. 8/20 for the baseline, 263 deletion interventions [75]), and complete-provenance repair cuts compromise to 0% (69.8–94.3% → 0% [76]; rollback repair 85.3% recovery vs. 77.3% for an LLM judge, with recurrence 26.6% vs. 12.1% [77]) — but both results are scoped to their stated decision/provenance conditions, and a governed bitemporal model meets its bounded contract (2,400/2,400 vs. 600/2,400) while disclaiming open-world accuracy [78]. Robust memory management more than doubles edit-acceptance in one study but remains incomplete, with no absolute acceptance numbers in the abstract relied on here [82]. **Shared terminology (both companion papers).** *Relaxation* is loss of availability (this section; availability drift in GyroMem). *Deletion* is the operation specified in this section; GyroMem's deletion operation is specified independently in its §Forgetting and does not inherit support closure or receipts. *Forgetting* in user-facing copy means only the deletion operation. *Verified* means measured by a stated procedure with a reported rate, never asserted. *Certificate* means the self-issued, unsigned, return-only artifact specified above. *Authority* means the (origin, act_class) pair of §2.6.

### Threat model (conditions every security claim in this paper)

**Adversary.** The attacker controls untrusted_external content: tool outputs, files, web pages, pasted text, and any record whose origin is not an authenticated channel. The attacker knows the defense design (Kerckhoffs-style) and has a bounded query budget, fixed in Phase 0. The attacker's goals, in scope: (a) causing a consequential action (tool call, state change, message sent) on the attacker's behalf; (b) deletion evasion (a deleted fact remaining recoverable); (c) exfiltration across principals (one user's records influencing another user's answers). Out of scope as attacker goals in this paper: (d) answer bias — untrusted memory coloring non-consequential answers (Louck's stated limitation, adopted here); (e) joins and in-context reconstruction — relations disclosed by combining records or by reconstructing a deleted fact from context (see the join limitation below); (f) cross-agent federation (see §8). **Coverage.** Each in-scope goal maps to a mechanism: (a) consequential action is governed by the Law V cap and the act gate (§2.6, §3); (b) deletion evasion is governed by the deletion operation, probe, and audit (§2.5); (c) exfiltration across principals is governed by per-tenant partitioning (built) and owner gating, with per-user isolation within a tenant a stated requirement, not a built mechanism (§3). No mechanism is claimed for the out-of-scope goals (d)–(f). Flooding and denial-of-service against the rent budget are not modeled in this paper: no mechanism against them is proposed, and Law I's budget is not a flooding defense. **Assumptions (A1).** The attacker cannot forge channel identity, break the cryptography the deployment uses, control the user-authentication channel, or write records bypassing the monitor. The trusted base is: the monitor (the component that assigns origin/owner and enforces the act gate), the audit ledger, the channels that authenticate origin, and deployer configuration. A compromised trusted tool is a residual risk: origin binding records what the channel delivered, it does not verify that a trusted tool's output is benign. **Dormant and sleeper attacks** are modeled as records whose payload is inert at write time and becomes consequential only after a later trigger (a future query, a consolidation, a compaction); the threat model covers them through the same origin/authority rules — a dormant record's authority never rises above its origin cap at any later stage. **MemPoison's L1/L2/L3 labels** are used in this paper only as pointers to [19]'s taxonomy; evaluation families in §5 are defined in plain language, not by those labels.

### 2.6 Law V — Authority Non-Creation (the security law)

*For any consolidation map C: {m_i} → m′, the derived record's authority is capped by its least-trusted source:*

$$\mathrm{Auth}(m') \;\le\; \min_i \mathrm{Auth}(m_i)$$

**Consolidation cannot manufacture trust.** Authority here is not a scalar: $\mathrm{Auth}(m)$ is the record's (origin, act_class) pair, ordered by the integrity lattice below, and the inequality means the derived record's pair is no higher than the lowest source pair. The name "Authority Non-Creation" states the rule: nothing is conserved — it is a no-creation cap. PPMF's [25] "provenance laundering" — where LLM rewriting upgrades an injected observation's apparent authority — is, in this theory, a *cap violation*, detectable as authority inflation: if Auth(m′) exceeds its least-trusted source, the operation is untrustworthy by definition. This turns a firewall heuristic into a law with a test: instrument any consolidation pipeline, measure authority flow, and flag violations. It also explains why write-time defenses fail MemPoison's [19] compositional and dormant attacks: they check content at write time, but the attack's payload is *authority*, which is created at consolidation time and detonated at read time. The law is stated for single-item derivation only: it governs one derived record from its sources. It makes no claim about joins, conjunctions, or retrieved sets (see the join limitation below), and it is extended by fiat — stated as a requirement, not derived — to precision updates and consolidation maps alike (§2.4). **Formalization.** Louck [35] provides the construction this law needs — with a bounded, machine-checked proof: a finite model (3,270 reachable states, at most 3 slots, sessions of length at most 2) with a machine-checked inductive step; the generalization to unbounded sessions is hand-argued, not machine-checked, and the theorem's scope is stated in the threat model above. Content-based detection and lineage-based provenance are malleable signals no defense can soundly rely on under laundering (T1); write-time origin binding from an authenticated channel is necessary (T2); non-malleable origin-bound authority with Sybil-resistant corroboration-gated elevation is sufficient (T3). The benchmark figures must be split by study, not merged: the unified study reports 0 consequential attacks in 192 trials per channel (95% CI [0, 2.0%]) at 100% legitimate utility; the cross-model trigger-style study reports 0 in 4,032 trials (95% CI [0, 0.1%]) at 95.9% legitimate utility (equal to its undefended baseline). The gate itself is measured at 1.3 microseconds in Louck's harness — a property of that implementation, not of any gate specified here. The guarantee covers consequential action, not answer bias; it does not cover in-context reconstruction or cross-agent federation; and it assumes correct origin labeling. The guarantee is conditional on the trusted monitor and append-only record described below: Louck's construction requires a trusted monitor, an append-only record, channel authentication (mutual TLS or OAuth with signed channels), single-use bound authorization tokens, and a hash-chained record. Mnemodynamics adopts the *rule structure* (origin binding, no-creation cap, corroboration-gated elevation) and states the monitor/record/token construction as a Phase-1 requirement — it is not built in the reference implementation (§3), and no claim in this paper should be read as adopting Louck's construction wholesale. The adopted item structure is Louck's M1: each record carries content, origin, scope, write time, and act_class — plus, in this paper, owner (authenticated principal id) and tenant id, assigned at write time by the monitor and write-once. Origins form a closed set — *user* (the authenticated principal), *trusted_tool* (channel-authenticated tool output), *agent* (agent-generated content), and *untrusted_external* (everything else; the fail-closed default). Scope is a deployer-defined permitted-use label with no enforcement semantics unless a deployment defines them. act_class ∈ {none, inform, act} is capped by origin on the integrity lattice: untrusted_external → none only; agent → inform only (an agent-generated record never claims act, so the self-summarization laundering Louck's construction excludes stays excluded); trusted_tool → inform, or act only when the tool's channel authentication and the deployment's policy jointly authorize it; user → inform or act. The conservative default is inform (none for untrusted_external). **The integrity order.** The pair order is total, so the law's minimum is always defined. Order act classes none < inform < act (capability), and origins untrusted_external < agent < trusted_tool < user (trust). Order pairs lexicographically, act class first: $(o_1, a_1) \le (o_2, a_2)$ iff $a_1 < a_2$, or $a_1 = a_2$ and $o_1 \le o_2$. The meet of a set of pairs is its lexicographic minimum; the join is its lexicographic maximum. The law's inequality is evaluated in this order: the derived pair must be lexicographically no higher than the lowest source pair. For a directly written record, the pair's origin component is the record's origin field. For a derived record, the monitor assigns the pair as the minimum source pair (or lower); the deriving principal is recorded in provenance, and the pair — not the origin field alone — governs the act gate. Scope does not enter the order: scope is a permitted-use label checked, if at all, by deployment policy at the act gate; it never raises the pair cap. Derived items inherit the no-creation cap. Elevation requires at least two independent principals (distinct identities in distinct administrative domains with no shared upstream source, as recorded in the monitor's registry) or fresh action-bound user authorization via a single-use bound token (tool, value, amount, nonce, timestamp; consumed on use; re-binding voids prior tokens). An elevated pair never exceeds the pair of the authorizing principal (for token elevation, the user's pair). Corroboration is counted from the monitor's record, not from record text: two records with the same upstream source, or two records the same principal authored, are one corroboration, not two. Sybil resistance rests on identity issuance by the deployment's identity provider (or monitor registry) — stated as a requirement; without an identity provider that makes principals costly to create, the two-principal threshold is bypassable by a single attacker creating two principals, and that residual risk is stated, not hidden. **Binding mechanism.** A text label travelling with LLM-rewritable content is lineage, not binding — it is the malleable signal T1 condemns. Binding in this account is the monitor-owned append-only record: the monitor assigns origin/owner/act_class at write time, records the assignment (with its hash chain) in a record the agent cannot rewrite, and the act gate consults the monitor's record, not the record's text label. Text labels are renderings of the monitor's record for legibility. The reference implementation carries labels with records and does not yet implement the separate monitor record; that is a Phase-1 requirement (§6), and any deployment that reads labels from record text alone does not satisfy this law. **Two senses of binding.** *Behavioral attribution* is the owner/origin information the model reads in the text: it affects model behavior (the [38] result) but is malleable and is never enforcement. *Enforcement binding* is the monitor-owned record: it alone governs the act gate. The first extension below concerns behavioral attribution; nothing in it weakens the rule that a text label is lineage, not binding. Three extensions follow from the newest evidence. First, **attribution must be visible to the model**: owner attribution bound into the credential line at write time cuts cross-user credential misuse from 92 to 1 of 305 paired runs, while trailing tags help only some models and separate metadata fields fail [38] — and owner-bound notes cost 3–5 clean-memory tasks per model — and a wrongly-written owner restores misuse, so owner fields are write-once from authenticated sources. Per-model trailing-tag numbers (Grok 28→0; Sonnet 31→28; gpt-oss 30→28) were not verified for this paper and are not stated. The stress-test scope matters: misuse nearly disappears when the user's own credential is present, so the 92→1 result is a misuse-under-absence result, not a general misuse rate. Second, a **join gap**, not a rule: the privacy of a retrieved *set* is not a function of its members — joins across individually benign records complete sensitive relations at retrieval [42] — so the retrieved set, with its cross-record links, is the unit of audit. No positive join rule is stated: the no-creation cap is insufficient for conjunctions, and MemJoin's evidence is privacy-only, not authority. Formulating a join rule is open work (Phase 0, §6); until one exists, join-auditing is logging only — it detects and records cross-record links, it does not prevent joins. The same limitation covers in-context reconstruction: a deleted fact re-derived from surrounding context satisfies the single-item cap while defeating deletion, and is out of scope for the deletion certificate (§2.5). Third, **judgeability**: a consolidation is Law-V-compliant only if the retained record preserves enough execution evidence to distinguish fulfillment from violation; identical records can correspond to compliant and violating executions, and three-valued verdicts (violation / no-violation / *undetermined*) must be first-class, never silently resolved [48]. The sufficiency criterion is CAVERT's *as a proposal*: the retained record must preserve the execution evidence sufficient to evaluate the verdict condition (Eq. 1 of [48]) — CAVERT's 8-tuple structure was not verified item-by-item for this paper, and the criterion is stated here as a design requirement, not as CAVERT's verified result. A record that cannot support the verdict is not Law-V-compliant. Policy under uncertainty: an *undetermined* verdict excludes the record from ordinary retrieval — it is flagged for review, never silently resolved, and restoration requires a fresh passing challenge — the system acts under uncertainty by withholding, not by proceeding. Compaction is inside this law, not beside it: any compaction that flattens typed messages into a single string with in-band labels manufactures authority through a deterministic code path (laundering succeeds 87–100% [36]), so role-typed or label-escaped serialization is required through every compaction boundary, and opaque residual compaction whose outputs cannot be inspected or receipt-checked is fenced as unauditable derived state that can never raise authority [50]. Fenced state is excluded from consumption entirely: it may be logged, but it must not influence act or inform actions. **Further security evidence.** The law's mechanisms now have independent support and sharper limits. Provenance-bound authorization cuts backchain attack success from 18.8% to 2.0% while preserving 92.1% legitimate-action success (vs. 11.0% for memory-side filtering) [59]. HMAC-chained provenance with randomized ablation cuts unsigned-content success from 93–100% to 0%, with authenticated single-injection success at 8.0% (95% CI [5.8, 10.9], n = 450) [62]. Machine-checked permission theorems with an endorsement gate report 0% observed attack success on the tested trojan in the authors' evaluation, under stated audit-scope limits [61]. Certified multi-source integrity admits no unsafe action in the authors' evaluation, using a minimum-hitting-set certifier [60]. Label-checking at the memory layer with "authority never lifts itself" as the design rule is the closest independent formulation of this law [57]. And the failure modes are now measured without any external attacker: false authority is accepted in up to 45.4% of endogenous cases with executors acting 99.0% of the time [64]; agents write misaligned goals to shared memory that aligned agents later carry out (58% with explicit goals, 18% values-only; persisting 100 sessions; removing the memory tool leaves 11% propagation via file-system fallback [68]); role confusion lets attacker preferences be recorded as the user's own [66]; composition violations rise from 35.02% to 58.45% across composed sessions, with a use-time guard cutting them to 6.67% in one recruiting-agent study [67]; synonym substitution evades a faithful-evaluation detector that otherwise reports perfect separation [63]; and simulated share-borne spread reaches 60–80% over 8 hops — simulation results, not production measurements [70]. Grounding awareness is the remaining gap at the law's boundary: a remembered proposal is not an instruction, and the remembered/instructed distinction must be preserved at retrieval [105]. Cross-user leakage is the law's cross-principal test: pooled retrieval leaks at 70–100%, and only hard ownership gating restores baseline behavior [65]. A wording-based defense is reduced to zero protection by LLM rewording, while a ledger verifying what actually executed is rewording-proof [58].

## 3. The epistemic immune system (design specification; built components stated separately)

Laws I–V describe the postulates. The immune system is the engineering: the set of mechanisms that would make the theory enforceable. Read-time challenge is one layer of it, not the whole defense. This section is a **design specification**. Where the reference implementation (v0.3.0, read-only status check November 3, 2026) already builds a component, that is stated; where it does not, the component is a requirement, and no claim in §8 rests on it as built.

**Formation constraints.** The formation process is the attack surface MemForm [37] isolates: five formation interfaces — Content, Evidence, Scope, Selection, and Path — with evidence authenticity checks, scope labels, retention logs (which experiences were dropped, and why), and fixed, verified consolidation order. A defense that only probes retrieved content at read time cannot detect Path or Selection poisoning, because the poison is in the process history, not the retrieved text — so the immune system includes formation-process probes, such as re-running consolidation under permuted order as a counterfactual (suggested feasible by [37]'s design, not demonstrated). Built: consolidation order and updater identity are logged. Not built: interface-level authenticity checks.

**Compaction-boundary check.** Every compaction/summarization boundary runs a structural, non-LLM provenance check: claims attributed to the user in the summary must match genuine user turns still held in harness memory, with ungrounded claims demoted (reported in [36] as catching 77% of laundering at 2% legitimate demotion on that harness; not tested against adaptive attack). Prompt-level defenses at this boundary are not claimed to protect it. Not built: no compaction-boundary structural check exists in the reference implementation.

**Read-time challenge.** Every retrieval returns memories with (content, precision λ, authority pair, provenance). Before consumption, each memory faces challenge. As built, challenge is a **heuristic probe suite** (its own documentation calls it heuristic): probes check authority inflation against declared provenance and consensus similarity across retrieved records, returning pass / undetermined / quarantine verdicts; the verdict is recorded and gates recall. A passing verdict grants at most +0.02 precision, capped at +0.10 lifetime. The suite does **not** generate counterfactual probes, does **not** measure probe-based surprise, and records question/source-turn presence and length only — never content. As specified here, the design adds: generated counterfactual probes ("if this memory were false, what would you observe?"), a challenge record with the same four outcomes (pass → precision up under Law III; fail persistently → quarantine; undetermined → flagged, never silently resolved [48]; signature of poisoning → authority inflation plus high surprise under probe), and a detection rule stated as a heuristic whose false-positive and false-negative rates are to be measured in Phase 2 — not as a verified detector. The detection rule *verifies* bound provenance against declared sources; it does not infer authority from content — read-time *verification* of write-time bindings is permitted, read-time *derivation* of authority from content is not (the distinction Law II's split requires). Probe surprise is scored on the same [0,1] predictive-failure scale as Law III's surprise (§2.4), by the challenge procedure; it is distinct from the §2.5 residual-recovery probe, which does not measure surprise. The flag threshold is set in Phase 0. The probe principal must be a separate monitor role from the agent whose memory is probed (a requirement, not a built separation); probe generation and scoring are the monitor role's responsibilities — the agent whose memory is probed never generates or scores its own challenges; where the same model substrate runs both, that is a stated limitation of the design, not a guarantee. Ground truth for a probe is the task outcome or environment feedback where available; otherwise the Phase-0 addendum must state the oracle used.

**Act gate.** Consequential actions pass a final check at the act boundary: the acting memory's authority must cover the action class, elevation must be corroborated (§2.6), and the verdict is written to an append-only hash-chained log whose chain detects interior edits and reordering but does not detect trailing-entry deletion, with no external anchoring implemented. The gate is a design requirement — act_class is bound, propagated, and logged in the reference implementation, but never enforced; no enforcement mechanism exists in code. No latency is claimed for this gate: the 1.3-microsecond figure belongs to Louck's harness [35], not to any gate specified here.

**Preview probes.** Before a consolidation or retrieval decision is committed, its effect is previewed against a baseline of pre-consolidation behavior, and only the harmful direction is penalized — one-sided, preview-based adaptivity, the design pattern that avoids the blanket-filtering tax [52] (weight-space evidence, analogical only; no memory-native end-to-end demonstration exists yet). Proposal only: not built.

**Benign costs are part of the specification.** Quarantine is reported with its costs on benign traffic — false-quarantine rate, token overhead, and accuracy delta, replicated (≥3 runs) — because read-time filtering that judges decontextualized fragments costs 4.4 points and touches 45% of questions in the scoped study [41]. Quarantine receives the question and the source turn (the Price of Safety authors' untested recommended fix, adopted here as a design requirement, not a measured result), and repeat-withholding of the same memory is rate-limited (rate set in Phase 0). Content-based quarantine is a secondary, explicitly non-guaranteed layer: under laundering it is malleable by theorem [35], and against formation attacks it is measured as nearly inert on the three content-screening defenses in MemForm's single-model evaluation [37].

**Credential auditing.** Credential handling is audited on three factors — exposure (E: which principals' records were returned to which requesters), behavior (B: what actions the consuming agent took on the basis of the record), and state (S: what persisted as a result) [38]. Per-user partitioning is the only exposure remover; binding alone does not remove exposure. Audit data is itself sensitive: it records which principals accessed which credentials, so its retention, access control, and deletion are requirements of the design, not afterthoughts. Join-aware auditing of the retrieved set (logging cross-record links, §2.6) and a self-propagation red-team (testing whether an agent writes goals its future self carries out, after [68]) are specified as Phase-2 evaluations.

**Implementation status (reference implementation, v0.3.0, verified read-only November 3, 2026).** Built: per-tenant partitioning (cross-tenant ids read as not-found; legacy files adoptable only by the default tenant; per-user isolation within a tenant is not built); server-side roles (owner/admin/auditor); generic OIDC single sign-on (implemented, not live-verified against any provider; bearer fallback off by default under OIDC); an MCP adapter (thin wrappers over the same operations); at-rest encryption scoped to record text and claim receipts under one global key (vectors, metadata, provenance, ledger, and transit are not encrypted); the fixed-reader residual-recovery probe (lexical/vector/keyword/aggregate content channels plus separate reference reporting) and the re-derivation audit (5 draws, recorded temperature parameter 0.2, real constructor; the temperature has no sampling effect under the deterministic constructor stub — see §2.5), run atomically before mutation on forget; the hash-chained audit ledger (with the tail-truncation and unsalted-hash limits stated in §2.5); single-bundle support receipts; and the heuristic challenge suite described above. Not built: the act gate (no enforcement), the compaction-boundary structural check, preview probes, certificate persistence/retrieval and HMAC signing (certificates are self-issued, unsigned, and return-only), per-tenant encryption keys, SQLite VACUUM/secure-delete, the separate monitor record for Law V binding, and any LLM-judge reader. Quarantine statistics are tracked; the false-quarantine rate is recorded as not measured. This paragraph states the status at submission; §8 states the gate.

Why this is not just "another filter": filters judge content; the immune system judges *provenance, process, and predictive performance under probe*, continuously, with memory of past challenges. Quarantine is reversible but bounded: a quarantined memory may be restored after a fresh passing challenge, subject to a maximum number of restoration attempts and a cooling-off interval, with owner or administrator confirmation as a design requirement — restoration is a status change via re-challenge, not Law IV decay (Law IV governs precision, not status). Nothing is deleted on suspicion — deletion is governed by the deletion operation (§2.5), not by the immune response.

## 4. Composition with GyroMem: shape × dynamics

GyroMem (companion theory, October 2026, revised November 2026) gives the *representation*: memories live on a mixed-curvature product manifold H^a × S^b × E^c, with radial position encoding abstraction and geodesic distance replacing cosine similarity. Mnemodynamics gives the *dynamics* — but no joint formalism exists, and the following table states only what is hypothesized, what is explicitly open, and what is cut:

| Mnemodynamics | GyroMem realization (status) |
|---|---|
| Precision λ(m) (Laws III–IV) | **Open.** Radial depth in GyroMem encodes abstraction, not precision. No mapping from precision to radial position is stated; none is claimed. |
| Reconsolidation (Law III) | **Open.** Point transport toward a query is a different operation from a scalar precision update. No map between them is stated; none is claimed. |
| Rent (Law I) | **Open.** No geometric rent is defined in GyroMem. |
| Authority (Law V) | **Open.** No authority field exists in GyroMem. |
| Immune challenge (§3) | **Open.** No claim that probe trajectories are geodesics is made. |

The geometric composition is a hypothesis, not a finding. A controlled test of geometric memory diagnostics finds that proximity features add nothing over question-difficulty and system-identity baselines on one diagnostic task (ΔAUROC +0.002, 95% CI [−0.010, 0.014], pooled AUROC 0.640 vs. 0.655 difficulty baseline, n = 2,367; on single-session-assistant questions the geometric classifier reached 0.841 vs. 0.826 for system identity alone, improvement +0.015, 95% CI [−0.020, 0.049]) [53] — proximity does not verify fact coverage and does not distinguish construction, retrieval, and generation errors on that task. That result is specific to Euclidean cosine geometry with a linear classifier on a diagnostic task; it does not falsify learned or mixed-curvature geometry, but it shifts the burden of proof: any geometric component must be evaluated as a *retrieval policy* (task-outcome gains over difficulty/system baselines and over explicit constraint channels such as TIDER's [49]) rather than as a diagnostic, and the theory states now what would change its mind (a controlled positive result on those baselines). Structure that succeeds in the surveyed work is explicit and outside the vector space — temporal windows [49], graphs with receipts [47], role-typed serialization [36] — not learned curvature inside it. Two architectural consequences follow regardless of geometry: compaction outputs that influence consequential action must be inspectable and receipt-bearing (the legibility requirement; REMORY's opaque soft-token residuals [50] are fenced accordingly), and the default compaction architecture is the persistent-graph pattern with per-request selection and revival of omitted blocks [51], which is reversible by design — with importance treated as a usage signal, never a trust signal. Native parametric memory [56] would require restating Laws I–V for state with no record to bind or delete, and is out of scope for this theory's claims until those restatements exist.

Neither theory needs the other — Mnemodynamics runs on flat vector stores, GyroMem runs with heuristic dynamics — and no composition is claimed: composition is future work, conditional on the §6 gates, requiring a joint formalism that does not yet exist. The product is described in §8.

## 5. What the theory predicts (and what would kill it)

**P1 — Read-time assembly dominance (scoped).** A read-time assembly policy (subset, cardinality, and order jointly optimized on downstream reward with a size penalty, in the manner of [45]) beats the strongest write-time consolidation baseline measured in the same harness in Phase 2 (baselines by implementation, version, and commit frozen in the Phase-0 addendum; no cross-paper pooling) on *transfer* (LongMemEval, split frozen in Phase 0, plus a transfer task requiring cross-session composition, scored on decision quality, in the spirit of [5]'s ConsolidationBench), at ≤2× the per-query token cost of the write-time baseline it replaces (build cost amortized over the benchmark's query count). Margin: ≥2 points on the full benchmark, paired bootstrap 95% confidence, α = 0.05. The claim covers assembly only: trust adjudication is excluded by construction (§2.3). **Kill:** no significant win at the stated margin on transfer → Law II's assembly claim is wrong and write-time curation is load-bearing for assembly.

**P2 — Precision beats Hebbian (untested prediction, not a finding).** Replacing EngramRAG-style [7] co-retrieval Hebbian updates with surprise-weighted precision updates (Law III) improves multi-hop decision quality on LoCoMo (split frozen in Phase 0) against the commensurable rival — outcome attribution [46] — head-to-head, from identical initial stores under a stated divergence protocol (stores diverge only through the update rule under test), with attention-EMA [51], recurrence-count maintenance [55], anti-persistence deletion [99], and category-conditioned retention [103] as design comparators run under the same protocol with their native operating points disclosed, with Kalman-filter and elastic-weight-consolidation style update rules as additional comparators specified from verified sources in the Phase-0 addendum before any run. No paper in the 100-paper reading basis directly compares these update rules; the nearest approach, attributed rewards, is correlational rather than counterfactual [46]. Margin: ≥2 points, paired bootstrap 95% confidence, α = 0.05. **Kill:** no significant win at the stated margin over the best rival → the surprise term is decorative.

**P3 — Adaptive defense (refined).** Formation constraints + compaction-boundary check + act gate + preview probes (§3) are evaluated against five attacker families defined operationally in the Phase-0 addendum: direct injection, compositional (multi-record), dormant (trigger-delayed), formation-stage (Path/Selection, after [37]), and compaction laundering (after [36]) — under an adaptive-attacker protocol in which the attacker knows the defense and adapts within the §2.6 threat model. **One bar:** attack success per family ≤ the same-harness defended-baseline attack success + 5 percentage points, where the defended baseline is the best write-time defense measured in the same harness, and ≤ 20% absolute in every family (the absolute ceiling binds whenever the relative bar would permit more, so a weak baseline cannot make a badly insecure system pass). The compaction family is additionally evaluated as a laundering rate on a pre-specified set of ≥20 harnesses with a reported confidence interval. Benign costs are a single budget, reported per deployed configuration: false-quarantine rate ≤5% per decision, accuracy delta ≥ −2 points (no upper bound — improvement is allowed), token overhead ≤3% (the 2.7% reranker-alone and 4.1% stacked figures from [41] are disclosed as exceeding this budget when stacked). No memory-native end-to-end demonstration exists yet; the weight-space analogue [52] is not evidence for the memory claim. ≥3 runs per family. **Kill:** any family exceeds its bar, or any benign-budget component is exceeded → the immune mechanism does not generalize.

**P4 — Deletion measurement (restated; no parameter-free formulation is claimed).** The original claim — relaxation matches EngramRAG's [7] reported scaffolding retention with zero per-type decay hyperparameters — is not made: no paper implements or validates parameter-free forgetting, and forgetting demonstrably requires machinery (receipts, support closure, verification [39], [40], [47]). The replacement prediction pre-registers the probe, reader, and analysis here, not in Phase 0: the fixed reader is the implemented hashed TF-IDF + lexical-rules reader (fixed-reader-v1) with four content channels — lexical (containment ≥ 0.5 with at least 3 matched tokens), vector (fresh-vector cosine ≥ 0.7), keyword (coverage ≥ 0.6 over the top 8 distinctive keywords), aggregate (union containment ≥ 0.5 with at least 3 matched tokens) — and reference survival reported separately, not counted in the rate; the re-derivation audit is 5 draws with the real constructor (draw count following the $R = 5$ structure of [47], §4), a matching criterion of token containment at or above 0.8 per sentence and per whole record, and a recorded temperature parameter of 0.2 with no sampling effect under the deterministic constructor stub; the acceptance suites are Bemula-style five suites (retention, efficacy, preservation, relearning, attack robustness, with paired controls [40]). Pre-registered acceptance criteria: forget-request pass rate ≥90%, preservation ≥95% on paired controls, and residual recovery ≤1% on the specified probe — on a text store within the stated scope (§2.5). The ≤1% figure is an **aspirational target**, set by analogy to PolyGraph's order of magnitude on a different metric — 0.4%/0.8% contaminated queries at 99.8–100% recall of a perfect maintainer on a knowledge-graph cleanup task with BM25 retrieval [47] — not on any MemCut-style probe, where the best measured result is 39–76% [39]; the analogy is motivation only, and the transfer from PolyGraph's metric to the residual-recovery metric is itself part of what Phase 2 tests. The single-bundle limitation (§2.5) is part of the prediction: the target applies to the single-bundle rule as built. This is a pre-registered *target*, not a result. **Kill:** any acceptance criterion unmet, or the residual rate not reported → the deletion protocol does not deliver its claim.

**P5 — Unlearning honesty.** Relaxation alone fails the retrieval-mediated portion of [14]'s behavioral unlearning audit (as the theory predicts); the deletion operation (§2.5) **alone** passes it — relaxation contributes nothing to passing the audit, and no combination is claimed — and every deletion ships with its measured residual-recovery rate — no bare "deleted" boolean. The field's current baseline (≤1.5% forget-request pass [40]; 39–76% residual recovery [39]) is the dishonesty this prediction is measured against. This is a *confirming* prediction of the theory's limit: forgetting ≠ unlearning. The audit scope is retrieval-mediated: parametric traces are excluded, matching §2.5's scope. **Kill:** if the deletion operation alone fails the retrieval-mediated portion of [14]'s behavioral audit, P5 fails. Separately, as a methods commitment: no deletion claim ships without a residual audit. **Multiplicity.** P1–P5 are tested at α = 0.05 each with Holm correction across the five predictions; the Phase-0 addendum is timestamped and frozen before any run.

## 6. Experimental plan (pre-registered)

**Phase 0 — Formal spec (1–2 weeks).** Write the five laws as an executable spec: tractable surrogates for surprise (the task-outcome score of §2.4), authority (the §2.6 origin/act_class order), and rent (the single-unit admission cost of §2.2). Phase 0 must also produce: the Law I admission cost in one fixed unit with its threshold; the reconsolidation map R(M, S, τ, u, q) with the updater domain U enumerated; the Law V item structure (origin taxonomy, scope, act_class caps, owner/tenant fields); the join-rule open problem, stated as a research task; and the deletion-protocol parameters (bundle semantics, audit draws, fixed reader — as pre-registered in P4). **Withdrawal rule:** a prediction whose operationalization Phase 0 cannot supply is withdrawn in the timestamped Phase-0 addendum before any run — it is removed from the prediction set, not suspended mid-experiment. No post-hoc suspension is permitted. Frozen deliverables: benchmark splits (LongMemEval, LoCoMo), baseline implementations with versions and commits, attacker-family definitions and the adaptive protocol, seeds, replicate counts (≥3), the compute budget, the judge/model conditions for every human- or LLM-judged measure, and a confound table (executor, judge, split, backbone) for every measure.

**Phase 1 — Prototype verification and extension (2–3 weeks).** Verification and extension of the existing Python reference implementation (flat-vector store, precision-update loop, relaxation scheduler, authority ledger, heuristic challenge): build the §3 design gaps (compaction-boundary check, act gate enforcement, preview probes, separate monitor record, certificate persistence and signing or their explicit absence), and extend the already-built deletion measurement components (reader probe, re-derivation audit) and per-tenant partitioning. Harness: LoCoMo subset + the five §5 attacker families + consumption eval specified in Phase 0 (decision quality + compliance + robustness, after [5], [20], [28]).

**Phase 2 — Evals + ablations (2–3 weeks).** P1–P5 above, each against the named baseline on identical infrastructure, with seeds fixed, ≥3 replicates, and the compute budget recorded in the addendum. Transfer measured by decision quality, not recall ([5], [20], [28]). Poisoning measured by attack success under the adaptive protocol (not just the attacker's published suite). Four additions: (a) the update-rule head-to-head for P2 (surprise-weighting vs outcome attribution vs attention-EMA vs recurrence counts vs anti-persistence vs category-conditioned retention, identical initial stores under the divergence protocol, counterfactual attribution required); (b) deletion verification for P4/P5 (MemCut-style residual probe + Bemula-style five suites with paired controls; residual rates reported, never booleans); (c) a geometry control experiment for §4 (geometric retrieval vs difficulty/system baselines and vs explicit constraint channels, with the pre-stated burden of proof); (d) two new evaluations — a join-aware retrieved-set audit (does the retrieved set disclose relations no single record states?) and a self-propagation red-team (does the agent write goals its future self carries out, after [68]?), plus a certificate-scope audit across image, embedding, and behavioral channels.

**Phase 3 — Composition (2 weeks).** Single gate: Phase 3 runs only if P1–P3 survive and the geometry control experiment (Phase 2c) is positive; P4 is excluded from this gate (deletion is orthogonal to geometry composition; a P4 failure blocks release under Phase 4, not composition under Phase 3). Port the dynamics onto the GyroMem manifold; test whether geometry × dynamics beats either alone, under the same frozen protocol.

**Phase 4 — Release (only if P1–P5 pass their §5 bars — P4's deletion bar and P5's audit bar included — and the Phase 3 geometry gate is satisfied).** Open-source engine, results publication, hosted API (§8). Timelines in this section are estimates, not commitments.

## 7. Honest ledger

**Borrowed (must cite):** the free-energy principle and predictive coding (Friston [29]), MDL/Occam (Rissanen [30]), variational inference, CLS theory [31] and reconsolidation neuroscience (Nader [32]), Hebbian plasticity, RRF, Matryoshka elasticity, artificial immune systems (de Castro & Timmis [33] — the challenge/quarantine pattern is not new in AIS, only its application to agentic memory), provenance and taint tracking (systems security), non-malleable origin-bound authority with bounded machine-checked guarantees (Louck [35] — the rule structure Law V adopts, including its conditional assumption A1: correct origin labeling from authenticated channels; the proof is bounded to a finite model with a machine-checked inductive step, and the unbounded generalization is hand-argued; the monitor/record/token construction is a Phase-1 requirement, not adopted as built), receipt-based deletion and support-closure auditing (PolyGraph [47]), residual-recovery auditing (MemCut [39]), five-suite unlearning acceptance (Bemula [40]), the memory-formation interface taxonomy (MemForm [37]), compaction-boundary structural checks (Compaction Laundering [36]), one-sided preview adaptivity (LookAhead [52]), provenance-bound authorization [59], HMAC-chained provenance [62], and machine-checked permission theorems with an endorsement gate [61].

**New (the bet):** (a) the five postulates as one operating account of agentic persistent memory, sharing a resource-constrained inference viewpoint, with kill criteria in §5 and the experimental plan in §6 — no in-window paper states write/retrieve/consolidate/forget as five postulates sharing one such viewpoint; (b) Authority Non-Creation as a formal law (PPMF [25] proposed the firewall; Louck [35] established the bounded, machine-checked non-malleability result; we propose the no-creation cap as the single rule, extended with the join gap and judgeability); (c) the layered epistemic immune system as a composition and operationalization of borrowed components (formation interfaces [37], boundary check [36], act gate [35], preview pattern [52], challenge [33]) with the authority-inflation + probe-surprise detection signature, as the adaptive defense for compositional, formation-stage, dormant, and compaction poisoning; (d) the deletion operation as a composition and operationalization of borrowed components (receipts [47], residual auditing [39], five-suite acceptance [40]) into the operational separation of deletion from decay; (e) the GyroMem × Mnemodynamics composition (geometry × dynamics), stated as an untested hypothesis.

**Why it might fail.** Free energy is intractable — if the surrogates (task-outcome surprise, single-unit rent, pair-ordered authority) are too noisy, the laws are untestable poetry; FEP has a reputation for explaining everything and predicting nothing, which is why §5 exists. Authority quantification needs provenance infrastructure that shipping harnesses lack ([24], [25]'s caveat applies to us). The Law V guarantee is conditional on Louck's A1 — correct origin labeling — and in real deployments the authenticated channel is often exactly what is missing [35], [36]. The deletion operation's scope exclusions (parametric model state, logs, caches, backups, model weights, federated or cross-store copies, aggregate vectorizer statistics, image and embedding channels, external copies held by callers) bound its guarantee; any claim beyond them is dishonest by the theory's own P5. The audit ledger records hashes and ids only, never record content — but the hashes are unsalted, so the ledger is a membership oracle for guessed content and must be treated as sensitive. Immune probes add per-query inference cost — the Jev-Mem [1] lesson (keep intelligence off the critical path) cuts against us, and P1's ≤2× cost bound is the tripwire — and benign quarantine costs [41] are now part of that budget. The ablations decide, not the math.

## 8. Product note: what the theory implies building

The theory implies a specific product: a **persistent memory layer for AI agents with audited deletion** — "memory with bounded, audited poisoning defenses — and deletion with a measured residual rate." The 2026 poisoning papers ([18], [19], [24], [25], [36], [37]) are market context, not market evidence: no market-size or willingness-to-pay claim is made here, and the desiderata in [5] (G1–G4) are research goals, not buyer evidence. The deletion gap ([39], [40]) is where the systems evaluated in [40] currently fail. Concretely: an open-source engine (the five laws as a library) for adoption, plus a hosted API with usage-based pricing and an enterprise governance tier — gated on the Phase 2/3 evals (§6). Built components of the reference implementation (v0.3.0) are: per-tenant partitioning (per-user isolation within a tenant is planned, not built), roles, OIDC single sign-on (not live-verified), the MCP adapter, scoped at-rest encryption, the fixed-reader probe and re-derivation audit, the hash-chained audit log (interior-edit evidence only; no tail-truncation detection, no external anchoring), single-bundle receipts, and the heuristic challenge suite. Planned, not built: the act gate with enforcement, the compaction-boundary check, preview probes, the separate monitor record, certificate persistence and signing, and per-user partitioning. Certificates as specified are self-issued, unsigned, return-only artifacts stating a measured rate and a scope — no independent attestation exists and none is claimed; no compliance certification (SOC 2, HIPAA, or otherwise) exists or is claimed. Two pricing facts from the newest evidence are disclosed, not hidden: a raw-record tier (no build cost) sits alongside the curated tier, with the break-even question count stated [43]; and quarantine is the expensive tier (token overhead 2.7% reranker-alone to 4.1% stacked, with accuracy and false-quarantine costs [41]). The engine composes GyroMem (representation) with Mnemodynamics (dynamics) as an option, not a requirement. A reference implementation exists and is tested (unit and integration tests against the built components listed above), built under an explicit override of the theory's original product gate (October 9, 2026): the gate was that no product be built until P1–P4 survived; the implementation was built anyway, and release remains gated on the Phase 2/3 evals (§6); a full build/release plan is held separately. The deletion claim it may make is exactly the scoped, measured one in §2.5 — no more.
